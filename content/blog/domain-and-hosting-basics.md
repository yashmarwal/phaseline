Three things sit behind every website: a domain, hosting and a security certificate. Owners often hear these words in a proposal and nod along without being sure what they mean. Because mistakes here can leave you locked out of your own website, it is worth understanding them. This guide explains each in plain language and tells you what to insist on.

## The domain

A domain is the address people type to reach you, such as yourbusiness.in or yourbusiness.com. You rent it from a registrar for a period, usually a year at a time, and renew it to keep it.

**Who should own it:** you. The domain should be registered in your business's name, with your email as the contact, and you should hold the login. If a developer registers it in their own name, they own the address your customers know you by. Getting it back can be difficult.

**Which extension:** .in suits Indian businesses, .com is widely recognised, and others exist. Choose one that is easy to say and spell, and avoid hyphens and numbers where you can. If a close variation is available, consider whether buying it protects you from confusion.

**Renewals:** set a reminder and enable auto-renew if your registrar offers it. An expired domain can disappear from the internet overnight, and your email can stop working with it.

## Hosting

Hosting is the computer where your website files live and from which they are served to visitors. There are several kinds, from shared hosting where many sites share a server, to platforms that deploy sites on a global network.

What matters to you:

- **Speed:** a good host serves pages quickly to your visitors.
- **Reliability:** it stays up.
- **Security:** it keeps software patched.
- **Ownership:** the account should be in your name, or, if the developer manages it for you, they should be willing to transfer it.
- **Support:** someone should respond when something breaks.

You do not need to choose the technology yourself, but you should ask your developer where the site will be hosted, who pays and what the renewal cost is.

## The SSL certificate

An SSL certificate is what puts the padlock in the browser and turns the address into https. It encrypts the connection between your visitor and your website. Browsers warn visitors about sites without one, and search engines expect it.

Many hosts provide certificates automatically and renew them for you. Ask your developer to confirm that yours is set up and will renew on its own. An expired certificate shows visitors a warning page.

## Business email

Many businesses use an address like name@yourbusiness.in, which looks more professional than a free webmail address. Email is connected to your domain, so if the domain lapses, the email stops. Keep both in your control.

## Common mistakes

- **The developer owns the domain.** Always register in your name.
- **Nobody knows who pays for hosting,** and the site goes offline at renewal.
- **No one tracks renewal dates.** Put them in a calendar.
- **Logins are lost.** Keep credentials in a shared password manager, not in one person's head.
- **Cheapest hosting chosen without thought for speed.** A slow site loses visitors.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a domain and hosting?

The domain is the address. Hosting is where the website lives. You need both, and they can be bought from different companies.

### Can I move my website to a different host?

Usually yes, if you own the files and have access. This is a reason to insist on ownership at the start.

### Do I need to renew my domain every year?

Domains are rented for a set period, usually a year. Renew before it expires to avoid losing it.
