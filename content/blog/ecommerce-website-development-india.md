Selling online is more than putting a price on a product page. A store is a set of connected parts: a catalogue, a cart, a checkout, payments, customer accounts, order records and delivery. This guide walks through each part so you know what you are asking for when you brief a developer, and which decisions are yours to make.

## First question: do you need a store at all?

Not every business that sells needs checkout. Wholesale and made-to-order businesses often close deals through conversation, and a catalogue with a quote request is a better fit. We explain that approach in [building a website for a wholesale business](/blog/wholesale-website-for-indian-traders).

A store makes sense when customers can decide and pay without talking to you, as in direct-to-consumer retail: clothing, home goods, packaged products and the like. Some businesses need both. A Jaipur ladies' wear manufacturer we worked with serves retail and wholesale from one store, with a switch between them; see the [Taj Attire case study](/blog/taj-attire-retail-and-wholesale-store).

## The parts of an online store

**Catalogue.** Products with names, photographs, descriptions, sizes or variants, prices and stock status. Good photographs and accurate descriptions do much of the selling.

**Search and navigation.** Customers must find things: categories, filters and a search that works.

**Cart and checkout.** The path from "add to bag" to "order placed". Every extra step loses some buyers, so keep it short and clear.

**Payments.** Indian shoppers expect UPI, cards, net banking and often cash on delivery. You connect a payment gateway to take them. See our [payment gateway guide](/blog/payment-gateway-for-indian-website).

**Customer accounts.** Let shoppers sign in, see past orders and check out faster. Accounts hold personal data, so they need careful security. See [customer accounts and login security](/blog/customer-accounts-and-login-security).

**Order management.** You need to see orders, update their status and notify customers.

**Shipping and returns.** Rates, delivery areas, courier integration and a clear returns policy.

**Legal pages.** Privacy policy, terms and conditions and a refund or cancellation policy are expected, and often required.

**An admin area.** Your team adds products, edits prices and processes orders without a developer. See [admin panels](/blog/admin-panel-for-your-business-website).

## Decisions that shape the project

1. **Which payment methods will you offer,** and will you accept cash on delivery?
2. **How many products,** and do they have variants such as size and colour?
3. **Who ships,** and how will you calculate shipping?
4. **What is your returns policy?**
5. **Do you need customer accounts or guest checkout,** or both?
6. **Do you also sell wholesale?**
7. **Who will photograph and describe the products?**

Answering these before you ask for quotes gives you more accurate prices and avoids surprises.

## Platform or custom?

You can build on a ready-made store platform or have a custom store built. Each has trade-offs, and they mirror the wider choice between a template and a custom build. ## Being found

An online store needs visitors. Product and category pages should have clear titles and descriptions, and the site should be fast on phones. Read [SEO for Indian B2B websites](/blog/seo-for-indian-b2b-websites) for the principles, which apply to stores too.

## Security matters more with a store

Stores hold customer details and take payments. Use trusted payment gateways rather than handling card data yourself, enforce strong logins, restrict who can read customer data and keep software updated. We reviewed exactly this for a cotton clothing brand; see the [QOTN case study](/blog/qotn-cotton-clothing-store-customer-accounts).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a payment gateway to sell online?

Yes, to accept cards, UPI and net banking on your site. Some businesses start with cash on delivery or manual payment, but a gateway is the standard route.

### Can I add a store to my existing website?

Often yes, depending on how your site is built. Ask your developer what is practical.

### How long does a store take to build?

It depends on the number of products and features. Agree a timeline in the proposal.
