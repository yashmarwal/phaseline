One of the first decisions in a website project is the platform. WordPress powers a very large number of websites, and it is a sensible choice for many. A custom-built website is the other common route. Neither is right for everyone. This guide lays out the real trade-offs so you can choose for your business rather than follow a trend.

## What each option is

**WordPress** is a content management system. You install it on hosting, choose a theme for the design and add plugins for extra features. A large ecosystem of themes, plugins and developers exists around it.

**A custom-built website** is designed and coded around your requirements. Rather than customising a general-purpose system, a developer builds exactly what you need, often as lightweight pages with an admin panel for the parts you need to edit.

## Where WordPress is strong

- **Familiar and widely supported.** Many developers know it, so finding help is easy.
- **Quick to start.** A theme and a few plugins can produce a working site quickly.
- **Good for content-heavy sites.** Blogs and publishing are what it was built for.
- **Lower starting cost,** especially with a ready-made theme.

## Where WordPress can hurt

- **Plugins add weight and risk.** Each one can slow the site and each needs updates. A neglected plugin is a common way sites get hacked.
- **Themes are general.** A theme built for everyone fits no one perfectly, and customising it deeply costs money.
- **Speed takes work.** A site with many plugins often needs effort to load quickly on a phone.
- **Maintenance is ongoing.** Core, theme and plugin updates must be applied regularly.

## Where a custom build is strong

- **It fits your process.** If you need a quote request tied to each product, a bulk order form or a member system, it can be built exactly that way.
- **It can be lean and fast.** Without the weight of general-purpose features, pages can load quickly.
- **Fewer moving parts to keep patched.**
- **You can own all of it.** The code and design are yours, and not tied to a theme licence.

## Where a custom build can hurt

- **Higher upfront cost** than a theme-based site.
- **You depend on the builder's quality.** A poorly built custom site is worse than a decent WordPress one.
- **Handover matters.** You must get the code, files and accounts, and an admin panel your team can use.

## A simple way to decide

Choose **WordPress** if:

- Your site is mostly content, such as a blog or a company profile.
- Your budget is tight and your needs are standard.
- You have, or can hire, someone to maintain it.

Choose **custom** if:

- Your site needs to do something specific to your business, such as bulk orders, quote requests tied to products or a member system.
- Speed and a distinctive design matter to you.
- You want a site your team can run without a stack of plugins.

## Do not forget ownership

Whatever you choose, ask who will own the files, the design and the logins. Get it in writing. With WordPress, you should also own the hosting account and admin login. With a custom build, ensure the code and any database are handed over.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is WordPress good for SEO?

It can be, but SEO depends on how a site is built, not on the platform alone. A well-built custom site and a well-built WordPress site can both do well.

### Is a custom website more secure?

Not automatically. Security depends on how the site is built and maintained. Fewer third-party components can mean fewer things to patch, but good practice matters either way.

### Can I move from WordPress to custom later?

Yes, though it takes planning. Content can be moved, and URLs should be redirected so you keep your search visibility.
