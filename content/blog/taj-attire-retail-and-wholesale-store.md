Taj Attire is a ladies' wear manufacturer in Jaipur. Like many manufacturers, it serves two kinds of buyer: people who want one piece, and businesses who want many. This case study explains the problem that creates for a website and how we solved it with a single store. We describe what was built and why, and we do not publish performance numbers for any client.

## The business

Taj Attire makes ladies' wear: kurtis, gowns and tops among other pieces. A manufacturer in this position can sell in two ways. It can sell retail, one piece at a time to individual shoppers. It can sell wholesale, in quantity, to boutiques and resellers. Each buyer wants something different from the website.

## The problem

Wholesale buyers searching for suppliers could not find Taj Attire, or see what the business makes.

That is a visibility problem and a presentation problem at once. A reseller looking for a ladies' wear manufacturer will check several websites, and the supplier they cannot find, or cannot judge, drops off the list. The business was making garments but had no online place where a buyer could see the range and decide to get in touch.

## What we built

- **One store with a retail and bulk switch.** A visitor chooses whether they are buying one piece or buying in bulk, and the store shows what is relevant to them.
- **A Royal Lookbook of full looks.** Instead of a grid of isolated products, the lookbook shows garments as complete looks.
- **Product cards with price and a Buy Now button.** A shopper who wants to buy can do so directly from the card.

## Why we made these choices

**One store instead of two websites.** Running separate retail and wholesale sites doubles the maintenance and splits the business's presence. One store with a switch keeps a single catalogue, a single brand and a single place to update.

**The switch respects what each buyer cares about.** A retail shopper wants to see a price and buy. A wholesale buyer wants to understand the range and ask about quantity. Letting each choose their path avoids forcing both through the same page.

**A lookbook sells what a grid cannot.** Garments are bought as outfits. A lookbook that shows full looks helps both kinds of buyer imagine the range, and helps a reseller see how pieces work together.

**Price and a buy button on the card.** For retail shoppers, the shortest path between seeing something and buying it is the best path. We kept it short.

## What other apparel manufacturers can take from this

If you sell to both consumers and businesses, you do not need two websites. You need a clear way for each visitor to say who they are and a catalogue that serves both. The bulk side should help a buyer see the range and ask a question, and the retail side should let a shopper buy without friction.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can one website serve retail and wholesale customers?

Yes. A switch between the two modes lets one catalogue serve both, while each visitor sees what is relevant to how they want to buy.

### Why would a manufacturer sell retail at all?

It can be a second channel that reaches shoppers directly. Whether it makes sense depends on your business, but a website makes it possible without a separate shop.

### What is a lookbook and why use one?

A lookbook shows products as complete looks rather than single items. For clothing, that helps buyers picture how pieces work together.
