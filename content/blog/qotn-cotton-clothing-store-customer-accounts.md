QOTN is a pure cotton D2C clothing brand from Jaipur, selling kurtas, shirts and dresses for men, women and kids at qotn.in. This is the story of the online store we built for it, and in particular the two things that made the project interesting: customer accounts that keep shoppers signed in, and a security check on how customer data is accessed.

## The business

QOTN sells directly to consumers. A direct-to-consumer brand depends on its own store, because every sale there is a customer the brand can know and serve, rather than a listing on someone else's marketplace.

## The problem

QOTN was a young brand that needed a store shoppers could sign in to. A store without accounts treats every visit as a first visit. The shopper types their details again, cannot see past orders, and has less reason to come back.

## What we built

- **An online store** for the brand's range of kurtas, shirts and dresses for men, women and kids.
- **Customer accounts that keep shoppers signed in.** A returning shopper does not have to log in again every time they visit.
- **A security check on how customer data is accessed.** We reviewed how the store reads and writes customer information, so that customer data is reachable only by the people who should see it.

## Why we made these choices

**Staying signed in is a convenience that drives return visits.** Anyone who has abandoned a purchase because of a forgotten password knows the cost. When a shopper stays signed in, coming back to buy takes seconds.

**Accounts are a promise about data.** The moment a store holds names, addresses and order history, it takes on responsibility for them. A login screen is easy to build. Making sure the rules behind it are correct is the part that matters.

**Checking access, not just appearance.** The security review focused on a basic question: for every piece of customer data the store holds, who is allowed to read it, and does the system actually enforce that? A store can look fine on the surface and still allow one account to reach another's records if those rules are wrong. Reviewing this before the store was in use is far cheaper than discovering it afterwards.

**Keeping the scope clear.** A young brand does not need every possible feature on day one. A store, accounts that work well and data that is protected are a solid foundation, and more can be added as the brand grows.

## What other brands can take from this

If you are launching an online store, put time into accounts and data access as well as the product pages. Ask your developer how customer data is protected, who can see it, and how that is tested. The answer should be specific, not "it's secure".

## Frequently asked questions

### Do online stores need customer accounts?

Not always, since some stores allow guest checkout. Accounts help when you want shoppers to return, see past orders and check out faster.

### What does it mean for a shopper to stay signed in?

The store remembers the shopper's session on their device for a period of time, so they do not have to enter their credentials on every visit.

### How is customer data protected in a store?

Through rules that control who can read or change each record, and through testing that those rules work. It is worth asking your developer to explain how this is done for your store.
