Trade shows and exhibitions are expensive. You pay for the stand, the travel, the samples and the staff time, and you come home with a stack of visiting cards and a head full of conversations. What happens next decides whether the event pays off. Most of the value is lost in the weeks afterwards, when follow-up is slow and scattered. Your website can help in three moments: before the event, at the stand and after you return.

## Before the event: make sure you can be found and trusted

Buyers who meet you at a show will often look you up that evening or later in the week. They are checking whether the business behind the stand is real. Before the event:

- **Check your website works on a phone.** That is how many will open it.
- **Make sure the range and key facts are online:** specifications, minimum orders and photographs.
- **Update contact details and your Google Business Profile.** See [Google Business Profile for service businesses](/blog/google-business-profile-for-service-businesses).
- **Put a clear WhatsApp and quote option on the site** so a warm contact can reach you instantly.
- **Reserve enough time to finish the work,** because last-minute launches are risky. See [how long a website takes](/blog/how-long-does-a-website-take).

## At the stand: connect the conversation to the site

Visiting cards and brochures are easy to lose. Make it easy for a contact to continue the conversation on their own terms.

- **Print your website address clearly,** on the stand and on materials.
- **Offer a QR code** that opens your catalogue or enquiry page. It lets a buyer carry your range in their pocket.
- **Keep a short list of the details you need** from each contact: name, company, what they need, quantity, timeline and how they prefer to be reached.
- **Write the key point of each conversation** on the card or in a note, while it is fresh. "Wants 500 pieces by March" is worth more than a name.
- **Ask permission to follow up,** and by which channel.

## After the event: follow up quickly and consistently

The days after a show are the window. Interest fades fast.

1. **Enter every contact in one place** within a day or two. A spreadsheet is a start; a tracking system with reminders is better. See [enquiry and lead tracking](/blog/enquiry-and-lead-tracking-system).
2. **Send a short, personal message** to each contact, referring to your conversation. Not a mass blast.
3. **Include a link** to the relevant part of your website, not just the home page.
4. **Offer a clear next step:** a quote, a sample, a call or a factory visit.
5. **Set follow-up reminders** for those who do not reply, with a sensible limit.
6. **Sort contacts by priority** so the warmest get attention first.
7. **Record outcomes,** so you can judge what the event produced.

## Use the website to qualify

A website can do part of the qualifying work. A buyer who has seen your range, minimum orders and process before they write is better informed than one who has not. A factory-visit booking option, for example, turns a vague "I'll come by" into a planned visit. We built one for a Jaipur screen printing house; see the [Tanish Creation case study](/blog/tanish-creation-bulk-order-website).

## Measure the result

Before the event, decide what success means: number of qualified contacts, quotes sent, orders won. After the event, compare. Over time you will see which shows are worth attending.

## For exporters

Overseas contacts add distance. Make your site easy to use in other time zones, with clear English and email and WhatsApp contact. See [websites for exporters](/blog/website-for-exporters) and [how overseas buyers evaluate suppliers](/blog/how-overseas-buyers-evaluate-indian-suppliers).

## A simple checklist

- Website tested on phone.
- QR code ready.
- Contact capture format agreed.
- Follow-up message drafted.
- Tracking list ready.
- Someone assigned to follow up, with a date.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I collect contacts on paper or digitally?

Whichever your team will actually do consistently. What matters is that every contact ends up in one list quickly.

### How soon should I follow up after a show?

As soon as practical, within days. The longer you wait, the colder the contact.

### Do I need a special landing page for an event?

Not necessarily. A clear catalogue and enquiry path on your main site is usually enough, though a simple page for the event can help.
