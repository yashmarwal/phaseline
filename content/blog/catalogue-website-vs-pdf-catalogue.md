Many Indian traders and manufacturers still run their business on PDF catalogues. They are easy to make, easy to send on WhatsApp and familiar to everyone. But as a business grows, the PDF starts to hold it back. This guide compares a PDF catalogue with a catalogue on a website, so you can see where each fits.

## What a PDF catalogue does well

It is fair to start with the strengths:

- **It is quick to produce.** Export from a design file and you are done.
- **It travels.** You can send it to a buyer on WhatsApp or email.
- **It works offline.** A buyer can save it and read it later.
- **It needs no technical setup.**

For a small range and a handful of regular buyers, that may be enough.

## Where it stops working

**A PDF is not searchable by what a buyer cares about.** A buyer who wants a particular blend, size or finish has to scroll through pages. There is no filter and no search that understands your products.

**It goes out of date.** The day you add a product or change a specification, every copy already sent is wrong. Old versions circulate for months.

**Google cannot use it well.** A PDF in someone's WhatsApp does not help new buyers find you. A website catalogue, built with proper structure, can appear in search results.

**There is no next step.** A buyer who likes a product on page 14 has to go back to the chat, describe which one they mean and wait. The momentum is lost.

**It is invisible to you.** You never learn which products were looked at. A website can show you which pages buyers visit, which helps you decide what to promote.

**It is a weak signal of scale.** To a new buyer, a PDF looks like a small operation, even if it is not.

## What a web catalogue adds

- **Browse and filter.** Buyers can narrow the range by category, specification or use.
- **One source of truth.** You update a product once, and everyone sees the current version.
- **A quote request on every item.** A buyer who is interested can ask about that exact product in one step. For a fabric trader we worked with, every fabric has its own quote request that lands on WhatsApp. Read the [Mapps Creation case study](/blog/mapps-creation-fabric-catalogue-website).
- **Search visibility.** Product pages can be found by buyers searching for what you make.
- **Specifications on the page.** The details buyers ask about repeatedly are answered without a message.
- **Updates by your own team.** With an admin panel, your staff can add and change products without a developer.

## When a PDF still helps

A web catalogue does not make PDFs useless. Keep a PDF for:

- Buyers who specifically ask for a printable document.
- Trade shows and in-person meetings.
- A short, focused line card that points to the website.

The best approach is often to make the website the main catalogue and generate a PDF from it when needed, rather than the other way round.

## How to move from PDF to website

1. **List what buyers ask most often** and make sure those facts are on each product.
2. **Organise products into logical categories** the way buyers think of them.
3. **Use your own photographs.**
4. **Add a quote request or WhatsApp button** to every product.
5. **Plan who will update it,** and give them a simple way to do so.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I keep sending PDFs while I build a website?

Yes. Many businesses do both during the transition and slowly shift buyers to the website.
