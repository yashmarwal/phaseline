Search engine optimisation, or SEO, is the work of making it easier for Google and other search engines to find your website and understand what it offers, so that the right buyers see it when they search. For B2B businesses, SEO is less about huge traffic and more about being visible to the few buyers who matter. This guide covers the practical basics for Indian manufacturers, exporters and traders. No one can guarantee a position on Google, so we focus on what you can control.

## How B2B buyers search

B2B buyers search differently from shoppers. They use specific, practical phrases: a product with a material, a supplier in a city, a manufacturer for a particular use. They often search several times, compare sites and return later. Many are looking for something very particular, so the number of searches may be small while the value of each is high.

Start by writing down how a buyer would describe what you sell, in their words. Our guide to [keywords Indian B2B buyers search](/blog/keywords-indian-b2b-buyers-search) goes deeper.

## What to fix first

### 1. Make sure Google can find and read your site

Your pages should be reachable by links, and a sitemap should list them. Google needs to be able to see your content. If a page's content only appears after complicated scripts run, it may be missed. Read [how Google finds a new website](/blog/how-google-finds-a-new-website).

### 2. Give every page a clear title and description

The page title is the clickable headline in search results, and the description is the summary underneath. Each page needs its own, written for people, and describing that page honestly. Keep titles short enough to display and avoid repeating the same title across pages.

### 3. Use headings properly

Each page should have one main heading that says what it is about, and sub-headings that organise the rest. This helps readers and search engines.

### 4. Write for the buyer

Put real information on your pages: specifications, capabilities, processes, locations and answers to common questions. Thin pages with a few lines rarely help. Do not stuff keywords; write naturally.

### 5. One page for one purpose

Create a page for each product category or service that buyers search for, rather than cramming everything onto the home page. Avoid duplicate pages that compete for the same phrase.

### 6. Make it fast and mobile-friendly

Speed and mobile usability are part of how search engines judge pages, and they matter to buyers too. See [website speed in plain English](/blog/website-speed-and-core-web-vitals).

### 7. Use structured data where it fits

Structured data is code that describes your business and pages in a form search engines understand. It can help with how your site appears. See [schema markup explained](/blog/schema-markup-explained).

### 8. Claim your local presence

If customers visit or you serve a local area, set up a Google Business Profile. See [Google Business Profile for service businesses](/blog/google-business-profile-for-service-businesses).

### 9. Earn links from real sources

Links from relevant, genuine websites, such as trade associations, directories you belong to and partners, support your credibility. Avoid buying links in bulk, which can harm you.

## What not to do

- Do not buy guaranteed-ranking packages.
- Do not hide text or stuff keywords.
- Do not copy content from other sites.
- Do not make claims you cannot prove.
- Do not create dozens of near-identical pages.

## Track progress

Use Google Search Console, which is free. It shows which searches bring impressions and clicks, which pages are indexed and whether there are errors. Check it monthly.

## How long does it take?

SEO is slow. New sites may take days to weeks to be indexed, and meaningful ranking progress often takes months. Competitive terms take longer. Our guide to [how Google finds a new website](/blog/how-google-finds-a-new-website) includes realistic timings. Be patient and consistent.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can SEO guarantee the first position on Google?

No. Anyone who promises that is overselling. Good SEO improves your chances, but results depend on competition and many other factors.

### Do I need SEO if I get orders by referral?

New and overseas buyers will search for you or check your site. Being findable adds a channel and supports the trust of existing contacts.

### Is SEO a one-time job?

No. The foundation is built once, but content, links and monitoring continue.
