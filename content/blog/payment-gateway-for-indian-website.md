If you want customers to pay on your website, you need a payment gateway: the service that securely takes their card, UPI or net banking payment and passes the money to your bank account. Choosing one is a business decision as much as a technical one. This guide explains what to compare and how to set up payments safely. Pricing, rules and approval processes change, so always check current terms with each provider.

## What a payment gateway does

When a customer pays, they are redirected to, or shown a form from, the gateway, which handles the sensitive details and confirms the payment. Your website receives confirmation that the payment succeeded. You never need to store card numbers yourself, which greatly reduces your risk.

Several Indian gateways exist, including Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree and CCAvenue. Features, pricing and support differ, and new options appear over time.

## What to compare

**Payment methods.** Does it support UPI, cards, net banking and wallets? What about EMI or international cards, if you need them?

**Fees.** Gateways charge fees per transaction, and sometimes setup or annual fees. Ask for the full schedule in writing, including fees for different payment methods, refunds and failed transactions. Check whether tax is added.

**Settlement.** How quickly does money reach your account after a customer pays? Is there a minimum or a hold?

**Onboarding.** What documents are required, and how long does approval take? Business registration, bank details and identity proof are typically asked for. Rules vary by business type.

**Reliability.** Does the gateway have a record of uptime and a clear status page?

**Support.** Can you reach a human when a payment fails? This matters more than it sounds.

**Integration.** Is it easy to connect to your website, and does your developer have experience with it?

**Refunds and disputes.** How do refunds work, and how are chargebacks handled?

**Reporting.** Can you reconcile payments easily with your accounts?

## Cash on delivery and alternatives

Many Indian shoppers still like cash on delivery, especially for first purchases. It brings its own costs: returns, refusals and handling. Decide whether you will offer it, and for which orders. Some businesses limit it by order value or location.

For B2B, bank transfers and invoices remain common. A gateway is not always the right tool for large wholesale orders.

## What your developer should do

- Connect the gateway using its official, secure method.
- Make sure the website never stores card details.
- Confirm payments from the gateway's server confirmation, not just the customer's browser, so a payment cannot be faked.
- Record every order and payment in a way you can reconcile.
- Test the whole flow, including failed payments, cancellations and refunds.
- Show clear messages to the customer at each stage.

## Legal and policy requirements

Gateways usually expect your website to show:

- A privacy policy.
- Terms and conditions.
- A refund or cancellation policy.
- Contact details, including address.
- Clear product or service descriptions and prices.

## A short checklist

1. Decide which payment methods you need.
2. Shortlist two or three gateways and compare fees, settlement and onboarding.
3. Prepare your documents.
4. Make sure your site's policy pages are ready.
5. Have your developer integrate and test thoroughly.
6. Make a small real payment and refund it, to verify.
7. Reconcile payments regularly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a company registration to get a payment gateway?

Requirements vary by provider and business type. Check with the gateway you are considering.

### Is it safe to accept payments on my website?

With a reputable gateway and a correct integration, yes. The gateway handles the sensitive details.

### Can I add UPI payments?

Most Indian gateways support UPI. Confirm it in the provider's current documentation.
