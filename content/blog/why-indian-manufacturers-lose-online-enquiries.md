Many Indian manufacturers and traders make excellent products and still receive fewer enquiries than they should. The cause is rarely the product. It is usually a handful of avoidable problems in how the business appears and responds online. This guide lists the most common ones, drawn from the patterns we see across the businesses we have worked with, and offers a simple fix for each. It is based on observation, not on statistics, and we do not claim figures for how often each occurs.

## 1. Buyers cannot find you

A buyer who does not know your name will search for what you make. If your business has no website, or one that Google cannot read, you are invisible to them.

**Fix:** a website built with clear titles, headings and structure, and a sitemap. See [SEO for Indian B2B websites](/blog/seo-for-indian-b2b-websites).

## 2. The range is locked in a PDF

A catalogue sent as a file cannot be searched, is quickly out of date and does not appear in search results.

**Fix:** put the range on the website, with specifications. See [catalogue website versus PDF](/blog/catalogue-website-vs-pdf-catalogue).

## 3. Key facts are missing

Buyers want to know minimum orders, specifications and where you are. When they must ask for every detail, many do not.

**Fix:** show the facts buyers ask about repeatedly, on the page where they need them.

## 4. There is no easy first step

A website with a single email address, or a long form, makes enquiring feel like work.

**Fix:** offer a short form, a WhatsApp button and a phone number, and attach a quote request to each product. See [enquiry forms and WhatsApp buttons](/blog/enquiry-form-and-whatsapp-button-design).

## 5. The site is slow or broken on a phone

Most visitors arrive on mobile. Heavy images and poorly built pages lose them within seconds.

**Fix:** a lean, mobile-first build, tested on real devices. See [website speed in plain English](/blog/website-speed-and-core-web-vitals).

## 6. The business does not look real

Stock photos, no address, no named people and vague claims make buyers wonder who is behind the site.

**Fix:** real photographs, full contact details and honest descriptions. See [trust signals](/blog/trust-signals-on-a-b2b-website).

## 7. Replies are slow

Buyers contact several suppliers at once. The first useful answer often wins. An enquiry that waits two days may already be lost.

**Fix:** agree a reply time, name an owner for each enquiry and make sure notifications reach the people who act on them.

## 8. Enquiries get lost between channels

Messages arrive on WhatsApp, email, calls and forms. Some are never answered or followed up.

**Fix:** a single place that records every enquiry with status, notes and reminders. See [enquiry and lead tracking](/blog/enquiry-and-lead-tracking-system).

## 9. The website is out of date

Old products, wrong prices or phone numbers that no longer work damage trust.

**Fix:** make sure your team can update the site themselves, through an admin panel. See [admin panels](/blog/admin-panel-for-your-business-website).

## 10. Nobody checks that the forms work

A form that silently fails costs enquiries without any sign.

**Fix:** test it regularly, send to more than one place and consider a care plan that includes checks. See [website maintenance](/blog/website-maintenance-and-care).

## 11. Overseas buyers are not considered

Buyers in other time zones need to browse and enquire without a phone call.

**Fix:** asynchronous contact, clear English and a full online catalogue. See [websites for exporters](/blog/website-for-exporters).

## 12. The website describes you generically

If your site could belong to any company, buyers have no reason to choose you.

**Fix:** say plainly what you make, for whom and how you work, in specific terms.

## How to start

You do not have to fix everything at once. Pick the top three problems for your business and fix them first. For many manufacturers, the biggest gains come from showing the range and the facts clearly and making the first step easy. Our [client work](/blog/client-work-what-we-built-for-six-indian-businesses) shows how this looked for six real businesses.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where should a manufacturer start?

With a website that shows the range, the key facts and an easy way to enquire. Then improve speed, search visibility and follow-up.

### Is it worth fixing an old website?

If it is slow, hard to update or does not work on phones, a redesign may pay off. See [when to redesign](/blog/website-redesign-when-and-how).

### Do small manufacturers need all of this?

No. Start small and build what your buyers need. Even a simple, clear website beats none.
