Tanish Creation is a Jaipur screen printing house that has been printing fabric since 1959. Its customers are businesses that need printed fabric in volume. This case study covers the problem the business had, the website we built, and the reasoning behind each decision. As with every client story on this blog, we describe what was built and why. We do not quote sales or traffic numbers, because we have not published any for this client.

## The business

A screen printing house sells a combination of designs, colours and quantity. A buyer rarely wants "some printing". They want a particular design, in particular colours, in a particular volume, by a particular date. That makes the first conversation important: if it is slow or vague, the buyer moves on.

## The problem

Before the website, orders and quotes started with a phone call or a visit. Buyers could not see the designs online, could not see the minimum order, and had no way to book a factory visit beforehand.

Each of those gaps has a cost:

- **Buyers could not see the designs.** A buyer who cannot browse the design library has to ask what exists. The team then describes it, or sends photos, one conversation at a time.
- **The minimum order was hidden.** A buyer finds out the minimum only after the call. If it does not suit them, both sides have wasted time.
- **Factory visits were arranged ad hoc.** For a manufacturer, a visit is often the moment a buyer decides. Without a booking step, visits depend on whoever picks up the phone.

## What we built

- **A bulk order form.** The buyer picks a design, a quantity and a colour, and the request goes straight to the business on WhatsApp.
- **The minimum order on every design.** The buyer sees it before asking, so the requests that arrive already fit.
- **A factory-visit booking form.** A buyer who wants to see the work can ask for a time without a round of phone calls.
- **The full design library online.** Every design is browsable, so the first conversation can start from a design the buyer has already chosen.

## Why we made these choices

**The order starts from a design.** When the request names a design, a quantity and a colour, the team can answer with a price and a timeline. Compare that with a call that begins "what do you print?"

**WhatsApp is the destination.** The team already runs its business on WhatsApp, so a bulk request arriving there needs no new tool and no new routine.

**Showing the minimum order saves both sides time.** Some owners worry that showing a minimum order will scare buyers off. Bulk buyers plan around minimums anyway, and a buyer who sees the minimum and still asks is a better enquiry than one who finds out halfway through a call.

**A visit booking is a trust feature.** Inviting a buyer to see the factory, and making that easy, signals confidence. It also turns a vague "maybe I'll come by" into a planned visit.

## What other manufacturers can take from this

If you make things to order, ask what a buyer needs to know before they will send a serious request. For Tanish Creation the answer was designs, minimum order and a way to see the factory. Your list will be different. Put those answers on the website and give the buyer a single step to act on them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a screen printer need an online store with payment?

Not to start. Bulk fabric orders usually need a conversation about quantity, colour and delivery first, so a bulk order request that lands on WhatsApp is often a better fit than a checkout.

### Why show the minimum order publicly?

Because it filters enquiries. Buyers who cannot meet the minimum learn that immediately, and buyers who can are better prepared when they get in touch.

### Can a factory visit really be booked through a website?

Yes. A simple booking form collects the buyer's details and preferred time, and the request reaches the team to confirm.
