Phaseline began with real client work, not with a pitch deck. Before we were a company with a name, we were building things for businesses that needed them. This page summarises six of those projects in one place: what each business needed, what was built and what the pattern across them tells us. Each has its own detailed case study, linked below.

## The six projects at a glance

**[Mapps Creation](/blog/mapps-creation-fabric-catalogue-website)** is a Surat Lycra and knit fabric trader. Buyers flipped through PDF catalogues, and overseas buyers could not browse or ask for a quote. We built a searchable fabric catalogue with a quote request on every fabric, delivered to WhatsApp, and a wholesale partner page.

**[Tanish Creation](/blog/tanish-creation-bulk-order-website)** is a Jaipur screen printing house. Orders began with phone calls and visits. We built a bulk order form that sends requests to WhatsApp, shows the minimum order on every design, books factory visits and puts the design library online.

**[Taj Attire](/blog/taj-attire-retail-and-wholesale-store)** is a Jaipur ladies' wear manufacturer. Wholesale buyers could not find it or see its range. We built one store with a retail and bulk switch, a Royal Lookbook and product cards with a Buy Now button.

**[INS Overseas](/blog/ins-overseas-export-catalogue-admin)** is a Jaipur handmade leather and paper exporter. Overseas buyers could not browse or see minimum orders. We built a catalogue of seven ranges, a wholesale partner page, WhatsApp and email enquiry and a private admin the team runs itself.

**[QOTN](/blog/qotn-cotton-clothing-store-customer-accounts)** is a pure cotton D2C clothing brand. It needed a store shoppers could sign in to. We built a store with accounts that keep shoppers signed in and checked how customer data is accessed.

**[Fitness Future Gym](/blog/fitness-future-gym-membership-system)** is a gym in Nangloi, Delhi. Attendance and fees were on paper. We built QR check-in, fee reminders, an admin panel that flags what needs attention and a member dashboard.

## What the projects have in common

Looking across the six, a few patterns repeat.

**The problem was usually a missing step, not a missing website.** Buyers could not see the range. Customers could not sign in. Fees were not tracked. In each case, the business was doing real work but a part of the process depended on memory, phone calls or paper.

**WhatsApp is where the work happens.** For most of these businesses, enquiries land on WhatsApp. We built around that habit instead of trying to change it.

**Show the facts buyers ask for.** Minimum orders, specifications and designs appear on the page, so serious buyers can qualify themselves.

**Give the team control.** Where a business needed to change content or records, we built an admin the team could use without us.
