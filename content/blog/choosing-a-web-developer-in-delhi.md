Delhi has thousands of freelancers, agencies and studios that build websites, and from the outside they can look alike. Choosing well matters, because a website is a long-term asset and a poor choice is expensive to undo. This guide gives you a practical way to compare, and the questions that separate a reliable partner from a risky one. The advice applies wherever your developer is based, but it is written for business owners in and around Delhi.

## Start by looking at real work

Ask to see websites the developer has built, not mockups or templates. Open them on your phone. Check how fast they load, whether they look professional and whether the business behind each one is real. If a developer cannot show live work, ask why.

Better still, ask whether you can speak to a past client. A short call tells you more than a portfolio.

## Understand who you will deal with

Many agencies put an account manager between you and the people who build. That can slow things and blur responsibility. Ask directly:

- Who will I talk to during the project?
- Will I be able to speak to the person doing the design and the person writing the code?
- How quickly do you reply to messages?

A small team that you can reach directly often means faster decisions and fewer misunderstandings.

## The ownership questions

These matter most and are the easiest to skip.

1. **Will I own the website files, the design files and the logins at launch?** Get this in writing.
2. **Is the domain registered in my name?** It should be.
3. **Who owns the hosting account?** Ideally you.
4. **If we part ways, can I take the site elsewhere?**

A developer who is vague on these may be planning to keep you dependent.

## The scope and timeline questions

- What exactly is included: pages, features, admin tools?
- What is not included, such as photography, copywriting or payments?
- How long will it take, and what are the milestones?
- How many rounds of changes are included in design?

A good proposal answers all of these without being asked.

## The payment questions

- How is payment split across the project?
- What happens if I cancel part way through?
- Are there any recurring charges, and for what?

Fair terms are clear and tied to progress. A common structure is a portion upfront, a portion at a midpoint and the rest before launch. At Phaseline, unless a proposal says something different, the split is 30% as an advance, 40% when half of the work is done and 30% before launch.

## The after-launch questions

- Is any support included, and for how long?
- What does a change cost after that period?
- Who handles security updates?
- What happens if the site goes down?

Support is where cheap quotes often hide their costs.

## The technical questions, in plain terms

- **Will it work well on a phone?** Ask to see it on one.
- **How do you handle speed?** A good answer mentions compressed images and lean pages.
- **Will Google be able to read it?** Ask about page titles, headings and sitemaps.
- **How will my team update it?** Ask for a demo of the admin panel if one is included.

## Red flags

- No live work to show.
- Refusal to put ownership in writing.
- Pressure to pay everything upfront.
- A price that is far below every other quote with no explanation.
- Promises of guaranteed rankings. Nobody can guarantee a position on Google.
- Vague answers about what happens after launch.

## Compare in a table

Put two or three quotes side by side. List price, what is included, timeline, ownership, support and payment terms. Differences that looked small in prose often become obvious in a table.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I choose a freelancer or an agency?

Either can be excellent. A freelancer may offer lower cost and direct contact. An agency may offer more skills under one roof. What matters is the quality of their work, their clarity on ownership and who you will actually deal with.

### Does location matter?

For meetings and visits it can be convenient to work with someone in your city. Many projects run well remotely too. Pick on quality and communication first.

### How many quotes should I get?

Two or three, from developers whose work you have seen and who answered your questions clearly.
