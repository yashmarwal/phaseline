The most common cause of a delayed website project is not code or design. It is content. The developer is ready, the design is approved, and everyone is waiting for photographs, product details or a company description that nobody has written. This checklist helps you gather what you need before the project starts, so the schedule holds.

## Why content comes first

A website is a container for your information. Design that fits real content is better than design that fits placeholder text, and a developer who has your real material can make better decisions about structure. Gathering content early also makes you clarify what you want to say, which is useful in itself.

## About your business

- **Company name,** exactly as it should appear.
- **A short description:** what you do, for whom and where. Two or three sentences.
- **Your story:** how long you have operated, how you started, what you are proud of. Only include what is true and what you can stand behind.
- **Location:** full address, plus a map link if buyers visit.
- **Contact details:** phone, WhatsApp number and email you want published.
- **Working hours,** if relevant.

## Brand material

- **Logo files,** in the best quality you have, ideally vector or high resolution.
- **Brand colours and fonts,** if you have them.
- **Any existing brand guidelines.**

If you have no logo of quality, tell the developer early. It affects the design.

## Products or services

For each product or service, collect:

- **Name.**
- **A short description** in plain language.
- **Specifications** buyers ask about: sizes, materials, weights, blends, finishes or whatever applies to your trade.
- **Minimum order quantity,** if relevant.
- **Photographs.**
- **Categories** you want them grouped into.

If you have hundreds of products, a spreadsheet is the easiest way to share them.

## Photographs

Photographs do more for credibility than almost anything else. Gather:

- **Your products,** photographed clearly on a plain background.
- **Your facility, workshop or office.**
- **Your team,** if you are comfortable showing them.
- **Past work,** for service businesses.

Use your own. Generic stock images undermine trust. If you need photography, plan it early, because it can take time. See our guide to [trust signals](/blog/trust-signals-on-a-b2b-website).

## Proof and credibility

- **Clients you can name,** with their permission.
- **Testimonials,** only real ones with the customer's consent.
- **Certifications or memberships,** only those you actually hold, with documents to prove them.
- **Years of operation, numbers or achievements,** only those you can verify.

Never publish claims you cannot back up.

## Legal and policy pages

Depending on your business, you may need:

- **Privacy policy.**
- **Terms and conditions.**
- **Refund or cancellation policy.**
- **Cookie notice.**

Ask your developer which apply and who writes them.

## Decisions to make in advance

- **Who approves the work?** One person is best.
- **What should visitors do?** Call, message on WhatsApp, request a quote or buy?
- **Which three websites do you like, and why?**
- **Which competitors should you look different from?**
- **What do you want to be found for on Google?** See [keywords Indian B2B buyers search](/blog/keywords-indian-b2b-buyers-search).

## Access you will need

- **Domain registrar login,** if you already have a domain.
- **Hosting login,** if you have one.
- **Existing website details,** if you are redesigning.
- **Social media and Google Business Profile access,** if you want them linked.

Learn more in [domain and hosting basics](/blog/domain-and-hosting-basics).

## A tip for getting started

You do not need everything perfect on day one. Start with the essentials: company description, contact details, logo and your best products with photographs. The rest can follow during the build, as long as you agree when it is due.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who writes the text for my website?

Ask your developer. Some include copywriting, some expect you to supply it. Make sure the proposal says which.

### How many photographs do I need?

Enough to show each product or service clearly, plus a few of your facility or team. Quality matters more than quantity.

### Can I add content after launch?

Yes. If your website has an admin panel, your team can add and edit content any time.
