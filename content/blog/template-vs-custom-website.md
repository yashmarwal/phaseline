Choosing between a template and a custom website is one of the biggest decisions in a project, and it is often made on price alone. This guide explains what each option actually gives you, when a template is the right call and how to tell when you have outgrown one.

## What a template is

A template is a ready-made design. You choose one, add your logo, text and photographs and publish. Many website builders and platforms sell or give away templates. The structure and layout are decided by the designer who made the template, for a general audience.

## What a custom website is

A custom website is designed around your business. A designer and developer start from how you sell and what your buyers need, and build pages and features to match. The result belongs to you and does only what you need.

## The honest case for templates

Templates have real advantages:

- **Speed.** A template site can be live in days.
- **Cost.** The starting price is usually lower.
- **Good enough for simple needs.** If you only need a few pages and a contact form, a template may do the job well.
- **Low risk to try.** If you are testing an idea, a template lets you start small.

If you are starting a very small business or testing a market, a template can be the sensible first step.

## Where templates fall short

- **Your site looks like others.** The same layout appears across many businesses, which makes it harder to stand out.
- **They are built for a general case.** If you need a quote request on every product, a bulk order form or a booking system, a template rarely does that without extra plugins or workarounds.
- **Workarounds add weight.** Bolting on features can slow the site and make it fragile.
- **You work within someone else's limits.** Changing the structure beyond what the template allows becomes expensive.
- **Performance is not tuned to you.** Templates often include features you never use, which add to page weight.

## The honest case for custom

- **It fits how you sell.** Every part of the site has a reason to be there.
- **It can do things templates cannot.** Product-level quote requests, bulk order forms, admin panels and member systems are examples.
- **It can be fast.** You only load what you need.
- **It builds a distinct impression.** Buyers see a business, not a theme.
- **You own it.** The design files, code and logins are yours.

## Where custom costs you

- **Higher starting cost and longer timeline.** Typically weeks rather than days.
- **It needs a good builder.** A weak custom site is worse than a good template.
- **You need a handover plan.** Make sure you receive files and accounts, and that your team can edit what needs editing.

## Signs you have outgrown a template

- You keep asking for "just one more plugin".
- Your team works around the website instead of with it.
- The site is slow on phones and you cannot find why.
- You want features the template does not support.
- It looks like your competitors' sites.

## How to decide

Ask what your website must do in the next year. If the answer is "show who we are and let people contact us" and your budget is small, a template can be fine. If the answer includes anything specific to your trade, such as product-level quotes, bulk ordering or records your team manages, a custom build will likely pay for itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I start with a template and move to custom later?

Yes. Plan for it by keeping your content organised and owning your domain, so the move does not disrupt your search visibility.

### Is custom always better?

No. It is better when your needs are specific. For simple needs and small budgets, a template can be the right choice.

### How do I know a developer will build something good?

Look at their previous work, ask about ownership and handover, and talk directly to the people who will build it. See [questions to ask a web developer](/blog/choosing-a-web-developer-in-delhi).
