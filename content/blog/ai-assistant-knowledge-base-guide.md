An AI assistant is only as good as the information it works from. Give it vague, outdated or contradictory facts and it will pass those on with confidence. Give it clear, accurate, well-organised facts and it will answer like a well-briefed member of your team. This guide shows how to prepare that information.

## Think of it as briefing a new hire

Imagine you hired someone to answer customer questions on your behalf, starting tomorrow. What would you tell them? What are you happy for them to say, and what should they refer to you? The knowledge for an AI assistant is exactly that briefing, written down.

## What to include

**Who you are.** Company name, what you do, where you are based and who you serve. Keep it factual.

**What you offer.** Products or services, with the details customers ask about: specifications, ranges, minimum orders, typical timelines.

**How you work.** The steps from enquiry to delivery, in plain language. At Phaseline, for example, a project runs through five steps: discovery, design, build, review and launch.

**How to contact you.** Phone, WhatsApp, email, address and working hours, exactly as they should be given.

**Policies.** Payment terms, cancellation and refunds, support, delivery. If you have published policies, base the assistant's answers on them.

**Answers to common questions.** Collect the ten to thirty questions that buyers ask most. Write each answer once, clearly. If your team already answers the same question daily, it belongs here.

**Examples of work.** Short, accurate descriptions of past projects or products, only what you are willing to make public.

## What to leave out, or handle carefully

- **Anything you have not published or are not ready to stand behind.** The assistant should not reveal or invent it.
- **Prices you do not publish.** If you quote per enquiry, tell the assistant to say so and invite a request rather than guess a number.
- **Private data** about customers, staff or finances.
- **Promises,** such as guaranteed results or delivery dates you cannot guarantee.
- **Speculation.** If the answer is "it depends", write what it depends on.

## How to write it

**Be specific.** "Minimum order is 100 pieces" is better than "low minimums".

**Use plain language.** Write the way you would speak to a customer.

**One fact, one place.** Contradictions confuse the assistant. If minimum orders differ by product, list each clearly.

**Say what to do when unsure.** Add an explicit instruction: if the answer is not here, say so and suggest contacting the team.

**Add the questions in customers' own words.** Include how people actually ask, including shorthand and mixed Hindi and English where relevant.

**State tone.** For example, polite, direct and concise, without exaggeration.

## Include boundaries

Tell the assistant what it must not do. Typical rules:

- Do not quote prices that are not published.
- Do not give legal, medical or financial advice.
- Do not promise delivery dates or results.
- Do not speak badly of competitors.
- Always give a way to reach a person.

## Test before launch

Ask it the questions your customers ask. Ask it tricky ones, such as things you do not offer and things you have not published. Check that it says "I don't know" when it should. Fix the information, not the symptoms. See [AI assistant accuracy and risks](/blog/ai-assistant-accuracy-and-risks).

## Keep it current

Put a reminder to review the knowledge monthly, and whenever prices, products, policies or contact details change. An out-of-date assistant is worse than none, because it spreads old facts with confidence. Read real conversations: the questions it could not answer show what to add.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much information does an assistant need?

Enough to answer the real questions customers ask. Many businesses begin with a page or two of facts and a list of frequent questions, and grow it over time.

### Can the assistant learn from conversations automatically?

Some systems can, but for accuracy it is usually safer for a person to review conversations and update the knowledge deliberately.

### Should the assistant know my prices?

Only if you publish them. If you quote per enquiry, tell it to explain that and invite a quote request.
