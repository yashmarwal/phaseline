A website for business buyers is not a consumer shop front. The person looking at it is usually checking whether you are a safe supplier, whether you make what they need and how easy you are to work with. They are often comparing you with two or three others in the same session. Good B2B design makes that comparison easy and puts you in the right light.

## How B2B buyers actually use a website

Buyers do not browse for fun. They arrive with a task: find a supplier, confirm a rumour, check a detail from a call, or compare options. They scan, and they leave quickly if they cannot find what they need. In practice they ask a short list of questions, often without realising it.

1. **What exactly do you make or do?**
2. **Is this a real, established business?**
3. **Can you handle what I need: quantity, quality, location?**
4. **What would it be like to work with you?**
5. **How do I get in touch, and will anyone answer?**

Your website is a quiet salesperson answering those five questions, so design around them.

## Answer "what do you do" in the first screen

Say plainly what you make or provide, and for whom. Avoid slogans that could describe any company. A line such as "Lycra and knit fabrics for garment manufacturers" does more work than "Quality you can trust". Put the line where the visitor looks first, and back it with one clear next step.

## Prove you are real

Buyers worry about dealing with an unreliable supplier. Cheap ways to reduce that worry include a real address and phone number, your actual photographs, named people, a company description with substance, and examples of work. ## Show capability, not just products

Buyers need to know whether you can meet their requirement. Where it is true for your business, show the details that matter in your trade: specifications, minimum orders, materials, capacity and the markets you serve. The more of those questions the page answers, the fewer the buyer has to ask, and the more likely they are to send an enquiry.

## Make the first step small

A buyer who is not ready to place an order will still send a short message if it is easy. Offer a few routes: a WhatsApp button, a phone number, a short form and an email address. Keep forms to the essentials. Every extra field loses some people. See our notes on [enquiry forms and WhatsApp buttons](/blog/enquiry-form-and-whatsapp-button-design).

## Keep it fast and readable on a phone

Many Indian buyers will open your site on a mobile connection between other tasks. Large uncompressed images, heavy scripts and tiny text lose them. A simple, well-built page that loads quickly beats a spectacular one that does not.

## Structure the site around buyer questions

A typical B2B site does well with a small set of pages:

- **Home:** what you do, for whom, and a clear next step.
- **Products or Services:** the range, with specifications.
- **About:** who you are, where you are, how long you have been trading.
- **Work or Clients:** evidence of what you have delivered.
- **Contact:** every way to reach you.

Resist the urge to add pages for their own sake. Each page should answer a question a buyer actually has.

## Common mistakes

- Generic stock photos instead of your real facility or products.
- A contact page that is just a form, with no phone or WhatsApp.
- Claims with nothing behind them, such as "best in India".
- A catalogue locked inside a PDF download.
- A design that looks good on a desktop and breaks on a phone.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is a B2B website different from a consumer website?

A B2B website is built to earn a conversation, not an instant purchase. It emphasises credibility, capability and an easy first contact over checkout speed and impulse features.

### Do B2B buyers still use websites in India?

Yes. Even when the deal is finalised on WhatsApp or a call, buyers often check a supplier's website first to confirm it is a real business.

### Should a B2B website show prices?

It depends on your trade. Many B2B businesses quote per order because prices vary with quantity and specification. In that case, show what you can, such as minimum orders and specifications, and make the quote request easy.
