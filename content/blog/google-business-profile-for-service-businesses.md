When someone searches for a business near them, Google often shows a panel with a map, a phone number, opening hours and reviews. That panel comes from a Google Business Profile, a free listing you control. For businesses where customers visit or where location matters, it is one of the most useful things you can set up. This guide covers how to do it properly.

## Who needs one

A profile suits businesses with a real, staffed location that customers can visit or that serves a defined local area: workshops, clinics, studios, gyms, showrooms, offices and the like. Google has rules on what qualifies, and listings that do not follow them can be suspended. If you have no real premises or service area, a profile may not be appropriate. Read Google's current guidelines before you start.

## Set it up

1. **Sign in** with a Google account that your business controls, not a personal account of one employee who may leave.
2. **Search for your business** to see if a listing exists. If it does, claim it. If not, create it.
3. **Enter your details** exactly and honestly.
4. **Verify** your business, as Google requires. The method depends on your situation.

## What to fill in

**Business name.** Use your real name, as it appears on your signage and website. Do not add keywords or locations to the name; that breaks the guidelines and risks suspension.

**Category.** Choose the one that best describes your main business, and add secondary ones if they are accurate.

**Address and service area.** Enter the real address. If you serve customers at their location instead, define your service area and follow the rules for hiding the address where applicable.

**Phone number.** A number your team answers.

**Website.** Your real site address.

**Opening hours.** Accurate, including holidays. Update them when they change.

**Description.** A clear, factual description of what you do. No promotions or claims you cannot support.

**Photos.** Real photographs of your premises, your work and your team. Add them regularly.

**Services or products.** List what you provide, with accurate descriptions.

## Consistency matters

Your name, address and phone number should match everywhere: your profile, your website, your social pages and any directories. Differences confuse customers and search engines. Check your website's contact page against your profile. Our piece on [trust signals](/blog/trust-signals-on-a-b2b-website) explains why consistent details help credibility.

## Reviews

Reviews influence both rankings and decisions.

- **Ask genuine customers** to leave a review, and make it easy.
- **Never buy or fake reviews.** It breaks the rules and can get a profile penalised.
- **Reply to reviews,** positive and negative, politely and factually.
- **Do not argue.** If a complaint is fair, acknowledge it and say what you will do.

## Keep it alive

A profile that looks abandoned undermines trust. Each month:

- Check hours and contact details.
- Add a photo or two.
- Reply to new reviews.
- Review questions customers have asked.

## Link it with your website

Make sure your website matches your profile and links to it where useful. A clear contact page with your address, phone, hours and map helps both customers and Google. On the website, structured data can describe your business in a form search engines understand; see [schema markup explained](/blog/schema-markup-explained). Only publish details that are true and that you wish to make public.

## What not to do

- Do not use a fake or virtual address to appear local.
- Do not stuff the name with keywords.
- Do not create duplicate listings for the same location.
- Do not use a personal account you may lose.
- Do not forget to update after moving or changing hours.

## Does it help B2B?

For manufacturers and traders with a physical location that buyers visit, yes, because buyers often check maps and reviews before visiting. For businesses without a visited location, it matters less. Either way, a profile is one piece of [SEO for Indian B2B websites](/blog/seo-for-indian-b2b-websites).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes, creating and managing one is free.

### Can I have a profile without a shop?

It depends on Google's rules. Service-area businesses can qualify in some cases. Check the current guidelines.

### How long does verification take?

It varies by method and situation. Follow the steps Google gives you in the dashboard.
