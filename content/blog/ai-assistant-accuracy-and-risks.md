An AI assistant speaks to your customers in your name. That is useful when it is right and costly when it is wrong. Most of the risks are manageable, but only if you know what they are and ask the right questions before launch. This guide is a plain list of the main risks and the safeguards that matter.

## Risk 1: It says something wrong

AI language systems can produce fluent answers that are incorrect. This is often called hallucination. It is more likely when the assistant lacks the information and tries to fill the gap.

**Safeguards:**
- Give it accurate, specific information about your business. See [what to put in an AI assistant's knowledge](/blog/ai-assistant-knowledge-base-guide).
- Instruct it to say it does not know when the answer is not in its information.
- Prefer stored, approved answers for the most important questions, such as policies and contact details.
- Test with hard questions before launch, including ones you cannot answer.
- Review real conversations regularly.

## Risk 2: It over-promises

An assistant eager to be helpful may imply guarantees, quote prices or promise deadlines.

**Safeguards:**
- Write explicit limits: no prices unless published, no delivery promises, no guaranteed outcomes.
- Make sure it points to a person for quotes and commitments.

## Risk 3: Privacy

Visitors may type names, phone numbers and business details into a chat. That information has to be handled responsibly.

**Safeguards:**
- Tell visitors they are talking to an AI.
- Say in your privacy policy what is stored, why and for how long. At Phaseline, for example, chats with Phase AI are stored in a private database and a weekly chat file is emailed to the team; this is described on our privacy page.
- Limit who can read stored conversations.
- Do not ask for sensitive details the assistant does not need.
- Choose providers you can trust, and understand where data goes.

## Risk 4: It goes out of date

Prices, products and policies change. An assistant that still describes last year's offer misleads customers.

**Safeguards:**
- Review its information on a schedule and whenever something changes.
- Keep one source of truth, and update the assistant from it.

## Risk 5: It frustrates people

A chatbot that loops, misunderstands or hides the human option annoys the visitors you want.

**Safeguards:**
- Always show how to reach a person, such as WhatsApp or a phone number.
- Keep the assistant optional, not blocking the page.
- Keep its answers short and clear.

## Risk 6: Misuse

Some visitors will try to make the assistant say unwanted things or ignore its rules.

**Safeguards:**
- Keep its instructions tight and its scope narrow.
- Limit what it can do. An assistant that only answers questions and collects details has little to misuse.
- Monitor conversations for unusual activity.

## Risk 7: Dependence on one provider

If your assistant relies on a third-party AI service, price or policy changes could affect you.

**Safeguards:**
- Ask which services it relies on and what happens if they change.
- Keep ownership of your knowledge and conversation data so you can move.

## Questions to ask any provider

1. How do you stop it making things up?
2. What happens when it does not know the answer?
3. Where are conversations stored, and who can read them?
4. Can I see and edit what it knows?
5. How do I review real conversations?
6. What limits can I set?
7. Who owns the data and the knowledge?
8. How is it kept up to date?

A provider who answers these clearly is more trustworthy than one who promises perfection.

## Being honest with customers

Tell visitors they are chatting with an AI, and make it easy to reach a person. People forgive an assistant that says "I'm not sure, please contact the team". They do not forgive one that confidently says something false. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can an AI assistant be made 100% accurate?

No system can promise that. You can reduce errors substantially with good information, firm limits, approved answers for critical questions and regular review.

### Do I need to tell customers it is AI?

It is good practice and builds trust. Make it clear in the chat itself.

### Is it safe to let an AI handle enquiries?

For first questions and collecting details, with clear limits and human follow-up, it can be. Keep people in charge of quotes, commitments and sensitive matters.
