An overseas buyer who has never met you must decide whether to trust you with money and a shipment from another country. They cannot judge your factory or handshake. They judge what they can see: your website, your communication and the small signals of reliability. This guide describes how that evaluation tends to go, so you can present your business well. It reflects general buying behaviour and our experience of building export catalogues, not survey data.

## Stage 1: discovery

Buyers find suppliers through search engines, trade platforms, referrals, exhibitions and social media. Wherever they first see your name, the next step is usually the same: they look for your website.

**What helps:** a website that Google can read, a clear company name, and consistent details across your profiles. See [SEO for Indian B2B websites](/blog/seo-for-indian-b2b-websites).

## Stage 2: the first look

In the first few seconds, a buyer asks: what does this company make, and is it relevant to me? They scan the home page. If they cannot tell quickly, they leave.

**What helps:** a plain statement of what you make and who you serve, clear product categories and real photographs.

## Stage 3: range and terms

Next they want to see the range and understand the basics: styles, materials and minimum orders. Buyers plan around minimums. If they must email just to learn it, many will not.

**What helps:** a browsable catalogue with the minimum order on each product. For a Jaipur exporter of handmade leather and paper goods, we put seven product ranges and minimum orders on cards with an Enquire button. Read the [INS Overseas case study](/blog/ins-overseas-export-catalogue-admin).

## Stage 4: credibility checks

Now the buyer looks for evidence that you are real and reliable. They may check:

- Your address and contact details.
- Whether photographs look like a real operation.
- How long you have been in business, if you say.
- Whether the website matches what you say elsewhere.
- Your Google Business Profile and any reviews.
- Whether the site is secure and professionally built.

**What helps:** real photos, full contact details, consistent information and honest claims. See [trust signals on a B2B website](/blog/trust-signals-on-a-b2b-website).

## Stage 5: first contact

If you pass, the buyer reaches out. They test you with a message and watch how you respond: how fast, how clearly and how professionally.

**What helps:** easy contact options (form, email and WhatsApp), a clear reply time that you keep, and a clean, polite and specific first answer. Read [enquiry forms and WhatsApp buttons](/blog/enquiry-form-and-whatsapp-button-design).

## Stage 6: the trial

Buyers often start with a small order or samples. They look at sample quality, packaging, documentation, communication during production and delivery.

**What helps:** realistic promises. Over-promising is the fastest way to lose a buyer after the first order.

## What overseas buyers worry about

- Quality that differs from the sample.
- Delays without notice.
- Poor communication across time zones.
- Hidden costs.
- Whether the supplier will still be there next year.

You can answer some of these on your website with clear processes, honest timelines and visible stability. Others are answered by how you perform.

## Make life easy for them

- **Write clear English.** Avoid idioms and jargon.
- **Use consistent units.**
- **Show a process,** from enquiry to shipment, in simple steps.
- **State how you communicate:** email, WhatsApp and typical reply times, only if you can keep them.
- **Offer samples or visits,** where it makes sense for your business.

## Keep the information current

A catalogue that is out of date wastes a buyer's time and damages trust. Give your team a way to update products and photos without a developer. See [admin panels](/blog/admin-panel-for-your-business-website).

## Your next step

If you export, review your site as a stranger in another time zone would. Can they see the range, the minimums, your details and an easy way to write? If not, start there. Our guide to [websites for exporters](/blog/website-for-exporters) lays out the details.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do overseas buyers still need to see a website?

Yes. Even buyers found through platforms or exhibitions usually check the supplier's website.

### Should I show prices to overseas buyers?

Many exporters quote per enquiry. If that is you, show minimums and specifications, and make quoting easy.

### How important is response time?

Very. Buyers contact several suppliers, and a fast, clear reply is a strong signal of reliability.
