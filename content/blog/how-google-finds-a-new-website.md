You launched your website, searched for your business name, and it is not there. This is one of the most common worries after a launch, and it is usually normal. Google has to discover your site, read it and decide what to show. This guide explains how that works, what you can do to help and how long things realistically take.

## The three stages

**1. Discovery.** Google has to learn that your site exists. It finds pages mostly by following links from other pages, and by reading sitemaps you submit.

**2. Crawling.** A program visits your pages and reads their content.

**3. Indexing and ranking.** Google decides whether to store the page in its index, and then, for a given search, which indexed pages to show and in what order.

Being discovered does not mean being shown. A page can be crawled and still not appear for the phrases you hope for.

## What you can do to help discovery

### Set up Google Search Console

Search Console is a free tool from Google that shows how it sees your site. Add your site as a property and verify ownership. A domain property covers every version of your address, including www and without. It is the most complete option when you can verify through your domain provider.

### Submit a sitemap

A sitemap is a file listing the pages you want found. Submit it in Search Console. Your developer should generate it so that it stays current. It tells Google what exists, and when it last changed. Do not list pages you do not want indexed.

### Link from other places

Links from your social profiles, your Google Business Profile and other websites help Google find you. See [Google Business Profile for service businesses](/blog/google-business-profile-for-service-businesses).

### Request indexing for key pages

In Search Console, the URL Inspection tool lets you check a page and request indexing. It is not a guarantee, and it is meant for a handful of important pages, not hundreds.

### Make sure nothing blocks Google

Check that your site is not accidentally telling search engines to stay away. A common mistake is leaving a "do not index" setting from the development stage switched on at launch. Your developer should check this before you go live.

## Common reasons a page is not indexed

- **It is new,** and Google has not got to it yet.
- **It is not linked** from anywhere, so nothing leads to it.
- **It is blocked** by settings or instructions meant for development.
- **It is a duplicate** of another page, so Google chooses one.
- **It is thin or low quality,** and Google does not see value in showing it.
- **It is slow or broken.**
- **Google chose a different address** as the main version of the page.

Search Console tells you which of these applies, page by page, in its indexing reports.

## One address per page

Make sure each page can be reached at only one address. Your site should send people from the non-www version to the www version, or the other way round, and from http to https. Mixed versions split signals and confuse search engines. Your developer should set a preferred address and redirect the rest.

## Realistic timelines

These are typical ranges, not promises.

- **Discovery and first indexing:** from a few days to a few weeks for a new site, faster when you have submitted a sitemap and have links pointing to it.
- **Title and description changes appearing in results:** often one to three weeks.
- **First rankings for less competitive, specific phrases:** weeks to a few months.
- **Meaningful ranking for competitive phrases:** several months, sometimes longer, and never guaranteed.

Expect your business name to be among the first things to appear. Generic phrases like "fabric supplier" take much longer than a specific one like a product with a city.

## What to do while you wait

- Keep publishing useful content.
- Make sure the site is fast and works on phones.
- Add your business to relevant directories and profiles that are genuinely yours.
- Check Search Console weekly at first, then monthly.
- Resist the temptation to change titles and structure constantly.

## When to worry

If a month after launch nothing is indexed, not even your home page, check that the site is not blocked, that your sitemap was accepted and that your pages can be reached without logging in. If problems persist, ask your developer or an SEO specialist to inspect the pages in Search Console.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long until my website shows on Google?

Often within days to a few weeks for indexing, though ranking for competitive phrases takes months. Submitting a sitemap helps.

### Should I pay for Google to list me?

You do not need to pay for organic listing. Paid ads are a separate option and not required to appear in search.

### Why does my site appear for my name but not my products?

Your name is very specific and easy to match. Product phrases are competitive and need strong, dedicated pages and time.
