A wholesale business runs on relationships, but relationships begin somewhere. Increasingly, that somewhere is a search or a link in a message, followed by a visit to your website. For a trader, the website is not a shop. It is a place where a new buyer decides whether to pick up the phone. This guide is about building one that does that job.

## What wholesale buyers want to know

Wholesale buyers think in terms of range, quantity and reliability. They are usually asking:

- What do you stock or make, and in what variety?
- What is the minimum order?
- Do you supply regularly, and to whom?
- How do I ask for a price?
- Are you reachable and responsive?

If the website answers these quickly, you spend fewer calls on first conversations.

## Show the range properly

List products by category, with the facts a trade buyer compares. For a fabric trader, that means blend and weight. For a garment maker, sizes and styles. Whatever your trade, find the two or three details buyers always ask about, and put them on every product. We did exactly this for a Surat fabric trader; details are in the [Mapps Creation case study](/blog/mapps-creation-fabric-catalogue-website).

## Offer a wholesale partner page

Someone who wants a sample order and someone who wants a standing supply arrangement are different buyers. A page written for the second group should explain how a partnership works, what you need from them and how to begin. It lets serious buyers self-identify.

## Be honest about minimums

State minimum order quantities where they apply. Some traders avoid this out of fear of putting buyers off. In practice, buyers plan their purchases around minimums, and one who sees the figure and still writes is a stronger lead.

## Use quote requests, not shopping carts

Many wholesale deals involve a conversation about price, quantity and delivery. A cart and checkout can get in the way. A quote request attached to each product, which sends details straight to your WhatsApp, fits how trade works. If you do want online ordering for repeat buyers, it can be added later.

## Separate retail from wholesale if you serve both

If you sell to consumers too, do not mix the two on one confusing page. A switch between retail and bulk, as we built for a Jaipur ladies' wear manufacturer, lets each visitor see what is relevant. See the [Taj Attire case study](/blog/taj-attire-retail-and-wholesale-store).

## Make updates easy

A trader's range changes with the season and the stock. Make sure someone on your team can update the website without a developer. An admin panel gives you that. See [what an admin panel does](/blog/admin-panel-for-your-business-website).

## Do not forget trust

New buyers want signs that you are established: your address, your photographs, how long you have traded, and examples of what you supply. See [trust signals on a B2B website](/blog/trust-signals-on-a-b2b-website).

## A starter structure

1. **Home:** who you are and what you supply.
2. **Catalogue:** the range, with specifications.
3. **Wholesale partners:** how to start a supply relationship.
4. **About:** your story, location and team.
5. **Contact:** quote request, WhatsApp, phone and address.

## What it costs and how long it takes

A trader's website with a catalogue and enquiry flow typically takes 3 to 4 weeks to build. Costs depend on the size of your range and whether you need an admin panel. Read our guide to [what sets website cost](/blog/website-development-cost-india).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a wholesaler need online payments?

Not necessarily. Many wholesale deals are agreed by conversation, so a quote request is often the right first step.

### How do I handle different prices for different buyers?

Quote per enquiry. A website can collect the buyer's requirements and send them to you, and you reply with a price suited to them.

### Can a website help me reach buyers in other cities or countries?

Yes. A website lets buyers who do not know you browse your range at any hour and ask for a quote without a phone call.
