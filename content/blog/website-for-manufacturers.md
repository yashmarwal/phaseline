Manufacturers often have the best product in the market and the weakest online presence. The factory is busy, the orders come through relationships, and the website is an afterthought or a template from years ago. But the first thing a new buyer does is look you up. This guide covers what a manufacturer's website should contain and how to make it work for a business that sells in volume.

## Know who is looking

The people who visit a manufacturer's website are mostly business buyers: merchants, brands, exporters, procurement managers and resellers. They are asking whether you can make what they need, at the quantity they need, to the quality they expect. They are rarely ready to order on the first visit, but they are often ready to ask a question.

## The facts buyers need

A manufacturer's website earns enquiries by answering practical questions. Where they are true for your business, include:

- **What you make**, with clear categories and photographs of real products.
- **Specifications**, such as materials, sizes, finishes and tolerances.
- **Minimum order quantities.** Buyers plan around them, and showing them filters out enquiries that cannot work.
- **Capabilities**, such as the processes you run and the types of work you do.
- **Where you are**, including your address, because buyers often want to visit.
- **How to start**, with a way to request a quote or book a visit.

## Show the factory

Photographs and video of your facility, machines and team are among the strongest trust signals a manufacturer can offer. Buyers who cannot visit want to see what is behind the product. Use your own photos, and show real working conditions. Polished stock imagery signals the opposite of what you want.

## Make it easy to request a quote

Most manufacturers sell on quotation, so the quote request is the most important feature on the site. Keep it short: what they need, how much, and how to reach them. Offer WhatsApp as well as a form, because that is where many Indian buyers prefer to talk. Our guide to [designing enquiry forms and WhatsApp buttons](/blog/enquiry-form-and-whatsapp-button-design) goes further.

## Offer a visit

A factory visit is often the moment a buyer decides. If you welcome visits, say so, and give buyers a simple way to ask for one. It signals confidence and helps you plan.

## Keep the catalogue online

Many manufacturers send a PDF catalogue. A PDF is hard to search, quickly out of date and invisible to Google. A website catalogue lets buyers browse, compare and enquire from the page they are on. We explain the difference in [catalogue websites versus PDF catalogues](/blog/catalogue-website-vs-pdf-catalogue).

## Let your team update it

A manufacturer's range changes: new designs, new materials, new photos. If every change needs a developer, the site goes stale. An admin panel lets your own team update products and content. That is a practical reason to consider a business system rather than a brochure site. Read about [admin panels](/blog/admin-panel-for-your-business-website).

## Think about being found

Buyers who do not yet know you will search for what you make. A site built with clear page titles, headings and structure gives you a chance to appear for those searches. Read our [SEO guide for Indian B2B websites](/blog/seo-for-indian-b2b-websites).

## A simple structure to start from

1. Home: what you make and for whom.
2. Products: the range, with specifications and minimum orders.
3. Capabilities or Process: how you work.
4. About: your history, location and team.
5. Contact: quote request, WhatsApp, phone, address.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do manufacturers really need a website?

Yes. Even if most of your business comes through relationships, new buyers and overseas buyers will look you up first, and a missing or weak website costs you those enquiries.

### Should I show prices?

Often manufacturers quote per order. If that is you, show specifications and minimum orders and make requesting a quote easy.

A typical website takes 3 to 4 weeks, depending on how much content and how many products are involved. See [how long a website takes](/blog/how-long-does-a-website-take).
