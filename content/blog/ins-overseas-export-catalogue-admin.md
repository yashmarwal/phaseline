INS Overseas is a handmade leather journal and paper exporter based in Sanganer, Jaipur. Its customers are wholesale buyers abroad. This case study describes the website we built so those buyers can see the full range, and the private admin that lets the team keep it current without us. We describe what was built and why. We do not publish enquiry or sales numbers for any client.

## The business

Exporters of handmade goods sell on relationships and on range. An overseas wholesaler wants to see everything the supplier offers, understand what the minimum order is, and decide whether the conversation is worth starting. Time zones and distance make a phone call a poor first step.

## The problem

Buyers abroad could not browse the range or see minimum orders without asking. The team also had no simple way to keep products and photos up to date.

Two problems sat side by side:

- **A visibility problem for buyers.** If you cannot see the products or the minimum order without sending a message, you may never send it.
- **A maintenance problem for the team.** A website nobody can update goes stale. New products and fresh photos are the lifeblood of a handmade exporter, and waiting for a developer for each change does not work.

## What we built

- **A catalogue of seven product ranges.** Each card shows the minimum order and has an Enquire button.
- **A wholesale partner page** for buyers who want an ongoing supply relationship.
- **WhatsApp and email enquiry**, so overseas buyers can reach the team the way they prefer.
- **A private admin.** The team uploads photos and manages products, the gallery and the homepage themselves.

## Why we made these choices

**The minimum order is on every card.** Wholesale buyers plan around minimums. Showing them up front saves the buyer a message and gives the team better enquiries.

**Two ways to enquire.** WhatsApp suits quick questions. Email suits buyers who want a written trail or who work in a company where messaging apps are not used for business. Offering both removes a reason not to get in touch.

**An admin the team owns.** We built the admin so the team can change the website without us. Photos, products, the gallery and the homepage are all in their hands. That is the practical meaning of independence: the site stays current because the people who know the products can update it any day.

**A separate wholesale partner page.** A buyer asking about one product and a buyer proposing a regular supply arrangement have different questions. Giving the second a dedicated page treats them seriously.

## What other exporters can take from this

Ask two questions. What does an overseas buyer need to see before they will write to you? And who will update the website when your range changes? If the answers are "the range and the minimum order" and "someone on my team", you have the outline of the project.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I update my own products on the website?

If your website has an admin panel, yes. In this project the team uploads photos and manages products, the gallery and the homepage through a private admin.

### Why show minimum order quantities publicly?

It lets buyers qualify themselves. Those who can meet the minimum contact you with a clearer idea, and those who cannot do not waste your time.

### How do overseas buyers contact an exporter through the site?

Through an Enquire button on each product, a wholesale partner page, and WhatsApp and email options.
