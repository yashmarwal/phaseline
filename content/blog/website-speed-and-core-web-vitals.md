Speed is the part of a website that visitors feel but rarely mention. They simply leave. In India, where many visitors browse on mobile networks of varying quality, a slow website costs real enquiries. This guide explains why speed matters, what Core Web Vitals measure and what actually makes a site faster, without technical jargon.

## Why speed matters

- **Visitors leave.** A page that takes too long to appear loses people before they read a word.
- **It affects trust.** A slow or jumpy site feels unreliable.
- **Search engines notice.** Page experience is one of many signals Google considers. It will not rescue a weak page, but a good experience helps.
- **It matters most on phones.** Mobile devices and connections are slower than a desktop on office broadband.

## Core Web Vitals in plain language

Core Web Vitals are three measurements Google uses to describe the experience of loading and using a page. Their details may be updated over time, so check Google's current documentation, but the ideas are stable.

**Loading: how fast the main content appears.** This is called Largest Contentful Paint. It measures how long it takes for the biggest visible thing, often a hero image or main heading, to show. Slow loading is usually caused by big images, slow servers or too much code that must run first.

**Interactivity: how quickly the page responds.** The current measure is called Interaction to Next Paint. It looks at how long the page takes to react when someone taps or clicks. Heavy scripts that keep the browser busy make a page feel sluggish.

**Visual stability: whether things jump around.** This is Cumulative Layout Shift. It captures annoying movement, such as a button shifting just as you tap it because an image loaded above it.

You do not need to memorise the names. You need a page that appears quickly, responds instantly and stays still while loading.

## How to measure

- **PageSpeed Insights** (free, from Google) tests a page and shows both a lab test and, where available, real-user data.
- **Search Console** includes a Core Web Vitals report, based on real visitors, for sites with enough traffic.
- **Your own phone.** Open your site on mobile data, not Wi-Fi. If it feels slow to you, it feels slow to buyers.

Remember that lab scores vary between runs. Look at patterns, not single numbers.

## What makes sites slow

- **Huge images.** The most common cause. A photo straight from a camera can be many times larger than needed.
- **Too many scripts.** Chat widgets, trackers, sliders and animation libraries add weight.
- **Heavy themes and plugins.** General-purpose tools bring code you may not use.
- **Slow hosting.** A weak server delays everything.
- **Unoptimised fonts.** Many font files or late-loading fonts cause delays and jumps.
- **No caching.** Visitors re-download things they already have.

## Practical fixes

1. **Compress and resize images.** Serve images at the size they display, in modern formats where possible.
2. **Load images only when needed,** such as below the fold.
3. **Reserve space for images and embeds** so the layout does not jump.
4. **Remove scripts you do not need,** and delay those that are not essential.
5. **Limit fonts** to what you actually use.
6. **Use good hosting,** ideally with a network that serves files close to the visitor.
7. **Keep the page structure lean.**
8. **Test on a real phone** after each change.

## Built fast from the start

It is easier to build a fast site than to fix a slow one. A site designed around your needs, rather than a general theme with many features, can be lean from the beginning. This is one reason people choose custom builds; see [template versus custom websites](/blog/template-vs-custom-website) and [WordPress versus custom](/blog/wordpress-vs-custom-website).

## A sensible expectation

Perfect scores are not the goal. A site that loads quickly on a mid-range phone, responds instantly and stays stable is a success. Focus on what buyers experience.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good page speed score?

Treat scores as a guide. Aim for a site that feels quick on a typical phone, and use the tools to find specific problems.

### Does speed really affect rankings?

It is one signal among many. Its larger effect is on visitors, who leave slow pages.

### Why is my site fast on my laptop and slow on my phone?

Phones have less processing power and often slower connections. Always test on mobile.
