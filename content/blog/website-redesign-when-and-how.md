A redesign is not a reward for owning a website for a few years. It is a business decision, and it is worth doing only when there is a clear reason. Done well, it fixes real problems. Done badly, it costs money, loses search visibility and leaves you with a site that looks new but works no better. This guide helps you decide whether to redesign and how to protect what you already have.

## Signs it is time

- **It does not work on phones.** If buttons are tiny, text overflows or pages are slow on mobile, you are losing visitors daily.
- **It no longer reflects your business.** You have new services, new products or a new positioning, and the website still describes the old company.
- **Your team cannot update it.** Every change needs a developer, so the content has gone stale.
- **It is slow and nobody knows why.** Old plugins, heavy images and outdated code add up.
- **It does not bring enquiries.** If visitors arrive and leave without contacting you, the page is not doing its job.
- **It is insecure or unsupported.** Outdated software is a risk.
- **It looks dated.** Visitors judge credibility quickly, and an old look can undermine trust.

If only one of these is true, a targeted fix may be enough. A full redesign makes sense when several are.

## What to keep

Do not throw everything away. Before the redesign, list what is working:

- **Pages that bring traffic.** Check which pages people actually find through Google. Their addresses and content are valuable.
- **Content that buyers like.** If your customers praise a section, keep the substance.
- **Your domain.** Keep it. Changing it adds risk.
- **Your brand.** A redesign refreshes how you present, not necessarily who you are.

## What to protect: your search visibility

The biggest risk in a redesign is losing the visitors you already have. Protect them with three steps.

1. **Keep a list of your current page addresses.** Export it before you start.
2. **Redirect old addresses to their new equivalents.** If a page moves, the old address should send visitors and Google to the new one with a permanent redirect. Otherwise people land on error pages and your rankings can drop.
3. **Carry over titles, descriptions and headings** for pages that already do well, unless you are deliberately improving them.

After launch, submit your sitemap in Google Search Console and watch for errors. Our guide on [how Google finds a new website](/blog/how-google-finds-a-new-website) explains this.

## What to improve

A redesign is a chance to fix the things the old site did badly:

- **Clarity:** say plainly what you do in the first screen.
- **Speed:** compress images and remove what you do not need.
- **Contact paths:** make WhatsApp, phone and a short form easy to find.
- **Trust:** show real photographs and details. See [trust signals](/blog/trust-signals-on-a-b2b-website).
- **Ownership and control:** make sure your team can update the content.

## How the process should run

1. **Review what you have.** What works, what does not, what buyers say.
2. **Define the goal.** What should the new site do better?
3. **Plan the structure and redirects** before any design.
4. **Design and approve** key pages.
5. **Build and test on phones.**
6. **Launch with redirects in place,** then monitor.

## What it costs

The cost depends on size and complexity, the same factors covered in our [cost guide](/blog/website-development-cost-india). A redesign is often priced like a new build, since most of the work is the same.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will a redesign hurt my Google rankings?

It can if addresses change without redirects or if content and titles are lost. Done carefully, rankings usually hold and can improve.

### How often should I redesign?

There is no fixed schedule. Redesign when the site no longer serves the business, not because a number of years have passed.

### Can I redesign in stages?

Yes. You can start with the pages that matter most, such as the home page and key product pages, and continue from there.
