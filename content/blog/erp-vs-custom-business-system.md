ERP is one of the most mentioned and least understood terms in business software. Vendors present it as the answer to everything. For some companies it is. For many small and mid-sized Indian firms, it is more than they need, and a smaller custom system aimed at their real problem is a better use of money. This guide explains the difference in plain language.

## What ERP means

ERP stands for enterprise resource planning. It is a large software package that tries to cover many functions in one place: accounting, inventory, purchasing, sales, production, HR and more. Everything shares one database, so a sale updates stock and accounts at once.

For a large manufacturer with many departments, that integration is valuable.

## Why ERP can be too much

**It is built for many industries.** To serve everyone, it includes modules and settings most firms never touch. Configuring it takes time.

**It asks you to adopt its processes.** Customisation is possible but can be costly and slow.

**Implementation is a project in itself.** Data migration, training and a period of running two systems at once are common.

**Pricing can be heavy.** Licences, per-user fees, implementation and support add up.

**Staff may not use it.** If the system feels like extra work, people return to notebooks and chats, and the data inside becomes unreliable.

A small firm that needs to track enquiries, attendance, orders or bookings may be paying for a lot it does not use.

## What a custom business system is

A custom business system focuses on the one or two areas where your business hurts. It replaces the registers, notebooks and scattered chats with a private platform designed around how your team works. It has an admin panel, records with status and notes, reminders and, where useful, WhatsApp and email notifications.

Rather than covering every department, it solves a defined problem well. For example, a gym needed memberships, fees and attendance in one place. ## How to tell which you need

**Choose ERP when:**
- You have several departments that need tightly linked data.
- You manage complex inventory, purchasing and production.
- You have the budget and appetite for an implementation project.
- Standard processes suit your industry.

**Choose a custom business system when:**
- Your pain is concentrated in a few areas.
- Your process is unusual, and standard software would force changes.
- You want something live in weeks, not many months.
- You want to own the code and data.
- Your team is small and needs simplicity.

**Use both** when your business is large enough: ERP for core accounting and inventory, and a custom tool for the part that differentiates you.

## A useful exercise

Write down the five most annoying things in your current process. For each, ask whether it happens because information is scattered, because tasks are forgotten, because reports take too long or because customers cannot reach you. If the problems cluster in one area, a focused system will probably solve them. If they spread across the whole company, a bigger platform may be justified.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a custom system a replacement for accounting software?

Usually not. Many businesses keep standard accounting software and add a custom system for operations that accounting tools do not cover.

### Can a custom system grow into something bigger?

Yes, if it is designed to be extended. Ask how new features will be added and priced.

### Do I need ERP to be taken seriously by large customers?

Generally no. Buyers care that you deliver reliably, not which software you use.
