A website for exporters: how to win overseas wholesale buyers

How an Indian exporter's website can reach overseas wholesale buyers: what to show, how to handle time zones and how to make the first enquiry easy.

For an exporter, the website is often the first and sometimes the only meeting with a buyer on the other side of the world. They cannot drop by the factory or call during your working hours. They find you, judge you and decide whether to write, all through what is on the screen. This guide covers how to build a website that works under those conditions.

What overseas buyers are doing

An overseas wholesale buyer is usually comparing suppliers across countries. They are looking for a reliable partner, a range that suits their market, terms they can plan around and a sign that someone will answer. They are also wary: shipping a first order to an unfamiliar supplier is a risk, so the website has to lower that risk.

Let them see the whole range

If buyers cannot browse your products, they cannot decide whether to contact you. A catalogue on the website, organised by product range, lets a buyer see everything you offer in a few minutes. This was the central problem for a Jaipur handmade leather and paper exporter we worked with: buyers abroad could not browse the range or see minimum orders without asking. You can read how we solved it in the INS Overseas case study.

Show minimum orders up front

Wholesale buyers plan their purchases around minimums. If they must write to you just to learn the minimum, many will not. Showing it on each product saves the buyer a message and gives you more relevant enquiries.

Give them a wholesale path

A buyer who wants a one-off sample and a buyer who wants a long-term supply relationship are different. A dedicated wholesale partner page, with the information those buyers need and a clear way to start, speaks to the second group directly.

Make contact work across time zones

Do not rely on phone calls alone. Offer contact methods that work asynchronously: an enquiry form, email and WhatsApp. A buyer can send a message at their convenience and expect an answer when your day begins. Say clearly how soon you reply, but only if you can keep that promise.

Be clear about who you are

Overseas buyers want to know your company name, location, how long you have been operating and who they would be dealing with. A real address, real photographs and a plain description of the business reduce the sense of risk. Our guide to trust signals has more.

Write for a global reader

Use simple, clear English. Avoid idioms and local shorthand. Present measurements in the units your buyers use, and state them consistently. If you serve markets in other languages, consider whether the key pages should be available in them, but start with excellent English.

Keep it fast everywhere

A buyer in another country loads your site from far away. Compress images, avoid heavy scripts and keep pages lean so the site feels quick. See our explanation of website speed.

Let your team keep it current

Exporters of handmade or seasonal goods change their range often. A private admin where your team uploads photos and manages products means the catalogue is always current, without waiting on a developer. Read more about admin panels.

Be found by buyers who do not know you yet

Buyers search for the products they want, in their own words. A website built with proper titles, headings and structure gives you a chance to appear for those searches. See keywords Indian B2B businesses should target.

Frequently asked questions

Do exporters need a website if they already use trade platforms?

A trade platform listing is useful, but you do not control it. Your own website is where you present your business your way and where you keep the relationship.

What should an export website include?

Your range, minimum orders, company details, a wholesale page and several ways to enquire, including email and WhatsApp.

Should I list export prices?

Many exporters quote per enquiry because prices depend on quantity and destination. If that is you, show minimums and specifications and make quoting easy.
