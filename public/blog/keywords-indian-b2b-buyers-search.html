Keywords Indian B2B buyers search, and how to choose the right ones

How to find the words Indian B2B buyers use to search for suppliers, how to match them to your pages and how to avoid chasing phrases that do not fit.

Keywords are the words and phrases people type into a search box. Choosing the right ones is the difference between a page that brings relevant buyers and one that brings nobody, or the wrong people. This guide explains how Indian B2B buyers tend to search and how to pick keywords that fit your business, using free and simple methods.

Think like the buyer, not like the seller

You describe your business by your own internal terms: product codes, trade jargon, brand names. The buyer starts with their need. A garment maker looking for fabric may type the type of fabric they want, a city, or the word "supplier". An overseas buyer may use different terms from an Indian one for the same product. The gap between your words and theirs is where most keyword mistakes happen.

The kinds of searches B2B buyers make

Product searches. A product plus a material, size or use. These are specific and usually show strong intent.

Supplier searches. Phrases with words such as manufacturer, supplier, wholesaler, exporter or dealer, often with a location.

Location searches. A product or service plus a city or region. Useful if buyers prefer a nearby supplier.

Comparison and question searches. "Which is better", "how to choose", "difference between". These are earlier in the buying journey, and a helpful guide can capture them.

Brand searches. People looking for you by name. Make sure you appear well for these.

How to find your keywords

Ask your customers and your team

What do buyers call your product? What do they ask on the phone? Your enquiries and WhatsApp chats are a goldmine of real language.

Use Google itself

Type your product into Google and look at the suggestions that appear as you type, and the "related searches" at the bottom of the results. Look at the pages that rank for the terms you care about, and note how they describe themselves.

Use Search Console

Once your site is live and indexed, Search Console shows which searches already show your pages. This is real data. Look for phrases where you appear but get few clicks; they point to pages and titles to improve.

Use a keyword tool, carefully

Paid and free tools estimate how many searches a phrase gets and how competitive it is. In B2B and especially in Indian niches, the numbers can be small, and that is fine. A phrase searched a few dozen times a month can still be valuable if each searcher is a potential customer. Treat estimates as rough guides, not facts.

Match keywords to pages

The principle: one main phrase per page, and a page for each distinct thing buyers search for.

  • Your home page targets your business name and overall offer.
  • Each product category or service gets its own page.
  • Guides and articles target questions and comparisons.

Do not try to make one page rank for everything. And do not create several near-identical pages for the same phrase, which makes them compete with each other.

Put the keyword where it belongs

Use your main phrase naturally in the page title, the main heading, the opening paragraph and where it fits in the text. Write for the reader. If a sentence sounds odd because of a keyword, rewrite it.

Avoid these mistakes

  • Chasing the biggest phrase without any chance of ranking for it.
  • Using terms you do not actually offer, which brings the wrong visitors.
  • Ignoring local and specific phrases, which are easier and more relevant.
  • Keyword stuffing, repeating a phrase unnaturally.
  • Forgetting that Hinglish and spelling variants exist. People type in many ways.

Check intent

Search for your target phrase and look at what appears. If the results are all shops and your page is an article, or all articles and your page is a shop, the page type may not match what searchers want. Match the type.

A simple process

  1. List ten ways a buyer might describe what you sell.
  2. Check each in Google and note the suggestions and who ranks.
  3. Pick one main phrase for each key page.
  4. Write the page properly, with real details.
  5. Track results in Search Console and improve.

Frequently asked questions

How many keywords should a page target?

One main phrase and a few closely related ones that arise naturally when you cover the topic well.

Are low-volume keywords worth it?

Often yes in B2B. A small number of highly relevant searchers can be worth more than a large number of casual ones.

Should I write in Hindi?

If your buyers search in Hindi or Hinglish, consider it. Check what they actually type, and test with real data.
