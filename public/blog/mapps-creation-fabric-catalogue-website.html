Mapps Creation: from PDF catalogues to a searchable fabric website

How a Surat Lycra and knit fabric trader replaced PDF catalogues with a searchable fabric website where every quote request lands on WhatsApp.

Mapps Creation is a Surat-based trader of Lycra and knit fabrics, selling to bulk buyers in India and abroad. This is the story of the website we built for them: the problem they had, what we built, and the thinking behind the choices. We have not published sales or traffic figures for any client, and we won't invent any here. What follows is what changed in how buyers find fabrics and ask for prices.

The business

A fabric trader's whole business is the range: dozens of qualities, blends and weights, and a buyer who needs to compare them quickly. Mapps Creation sells wholesale, so the people looking at the range are garment makers, merchants and exporters, not casual shoppers. They want specifications first and a price second.

The problem

Before the new website, buyers flipped through PDF catalogues. Overseas buyers had no way to browse the fabrics or ask for a quote at all.

That is a very common setup for traders, and it is easy to see why it stops working:

  • A PDF is a document, not a shop window. It has to be sent, opened, scrolled and remembered. Nothing in it is searchable by what a buyer actually cares about.
  • It goes out of date the moment it is sent. New qualities arrive, others sell out, and old copies keep circulating on WhatsApp.
  • There is no obvious next step. A buyer who likes a fabric on page 14 has to go back to the chat, describe which fabric they meant, and wait.
  • Buyers abroad are shut out. Someone in another time zone cannot call, and cannot browse. They simply move to a supplier who lets them look.

What we built

We built a fabric website and a wholesale partner page for bulk buyers:

  • A searchable fabric catalogue. Every fabric has its own entry with the specifications a buyer compares, including GSM and blend, so a buyer can narrow the range instead of flipping pages.
  • A quote request on every fabric. Each request goes straight to the trader's WhatsApp, which is where the team already handles enquiries.
  • A wholesale partner page for bulk buyers who want to set up a regular supply relationship rather than ask about one fabric.

The site is built to be used on a phone, because that is where many buyers will open it.

Why we made these choices

Enquiries go to WhatsApp, not to a form nobody checks. Indian trade runs on WhatsApp. A beautiful enquiry form that sends an email to an inbox the owner opens twice a day loses to a message that arrives on the phone already in the owner's hand. Putting the quote request where the team already works means the website adds no new habit to learn.

The quote button sits on the fabric, not on a separate contact page. A request that starts from a specific fabric starts from something concrete. The buyer does not have to describe a quality from memory, and the team does not have to ask "which one do you mean?"

Specifications are on the page. Buyers of fabric ask the same questions again and again: what blend, what weight. Answering those on the page removes a round of back-and-forth from every enquiry and lets serious buyers shortlist on their own.

A separate page for wholesale partners. Someone placing one trial order and someone setting up an ongoing supply relationship are different buyers. Giving the second group their own page speaks to them directly.

What this means for other textile traders

You do not need a giant store to sell fabric to businesses. You need your range presented in a way that can be searched, with the details buyers compare, and a one-step way to ask for a price. If you currently send PDFs, start by listing what buyers ask you most often. Those questions are the fields your website should answer.

Frequently asked questions

Does a fabric trader need an online store with payments?

Not necessarily. Many wholesale buyers want a quote and a conversation before they order, so a searchable catalogue with a quote request is often the right first step. Payments can be added later if your buyers want to order online.

Why use WhatsApp for enquiries?

Because that is where most Indian traders already talk to buyers. Sending requests to WhatsApp means they are seen quickly and answered in the same place the relationship already lives.

Can overseas buyers use the website?

Yes. A catalogue that can be browsed at any hour and a quote request that does not need a phone call remove the two biggest barriers for buyers in other time zones.
