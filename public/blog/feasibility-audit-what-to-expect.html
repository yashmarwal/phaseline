The feasibility audit: what a 30-minute call with Phaseline covers

What happens in a 30-minute Phaseline feasibility audit, how to prepare, what you receive afterwards and how it differs from a sales call.

Hiring someone to build a website or business system can feel like a leap, especially if you are not sure what you need or whether it is worth doing. A feasibility audit is our way of lowering that risk. It is a short, direct conversation to work out what makes sense for your business before any money changes hands. This page explains what happens and how to prepare.

What it is

A feasibility audit is a 30-minute call with the team. In that time, we review your current site or your plans and give you a concrete scope and timeline. It is complimentary, there is no sales pitch, and you speak directly with the people who would build the project, with no intermediaries.

What we cover

Your business and buyers. What you sell, who buys and how they find and contact you today.

Your current setup. If you have a website, we look at what works and what does not. If you have notes, registers or spreadsheets, we look at how your process runs.

What you want to achieve. More enquiries, easier updates, better records, selling online. We help you separate must-haves from nice-to-haves.

What is feasible. Whether a website, a business system or custom software fits, and whether your goal is realistic.

Scope and timeline. A clear picture of what would be built and how long it typically takes. A website typically takes 3 to 4 weeks, and a business system 5 to 8 weeks, depending on scope. Custom software is timed after a consultation.

Next steps. What would happen if you decided to proceed, and what a proposal would include.

What it is not

  • It is not a high-pressure sales call.
  • It is not a free design or a free build.
  • It is not a commitment. You can walk away with a clearer idea of what you need, even if you hire someone else.

How it differs from a consultation

Our consultation is a longer working session to review your current setup and agree a clear scope, timeline and next steps. The feasibility audit is the shorter first step: a quick, honest check on whether and how a project makes sense.

How to prepare

You do not need a polished brief. A little preparation makes the 30 minutes far more useful.

  1. Know your goal in a sentence. "I want buyers to be able to see our range and ask for a quote."
  2. List what frustrates you now. Missed enquiries, slow updates, paper records, an outdated site.
  3. Gather examples. Three websites you like and three you do not, and why.
  4. Have your current site address ready, if you have one.
  5. Think about your deadline, if there is one, such as an event or season.
  6. Have the decision-maker on the call. It saves time.

Our content checklist is useful once you move forward.

What you receive

After the call, you should know:

  • Whether a project makes sense.
  • The type of solution that fits.
  • A rough scope and timeline.
  • What the next step is.

If you proceed, you receive a proposal. We do not publish prices, because each project is quoted after a short consultation. See how we quote a project.

How to book

Use the contact page on our site, or the WhatsApp and email details listed there. We reply within 1 hour during working hours.

Questions people ask

We often hear: "I don't know what I need." That is fine; working it out is the purpose of the call. Others ask: "Will you push me to buy?" No. The aim is for you to decide with clear information. See also how a Phaseline project runs.

Frequently asked questions

Does the feasibility audit cost anything?

It is complimentary.

Do I need to prepare documents?

No, though notes help. Bring what you have.

Who will I speak to?

The people who would be working on your project, not a separate sales team.
