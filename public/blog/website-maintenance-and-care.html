Website maintenance: what it is, what it costs you to skip it and what to look for

What website maintenance covers, why neglected sites go down or get hacked, and what a monthly care plan for an Indian business website should include.

A website is not finished when it launches. Like a shop, it needs regular attention to stay open, safe and accurate. Many business owners learn this the hard way: a site that goes down for a day without anyone noticing, a contact form that quietly stopped working or a security problem found by a customer. This guide explains what website maintenance covers, what happens without it and what a sensible plan includes.

What maintenance actually means

Maintenance is the routine work that keeps a website healthy. It usually covers five areas.

  1. Uptime. Is the site actually available? A site can be down without anyone on your team noticing.
  2. Security. Software, platforms and certificates need updates. Outdated components are a common route for attacks.
  3. Content. Prices, products, photos, contact details and news change. The website should too.
  4. Performance. Pages should stay quick as content grows.
  5. Search health. Google's view of your site changes over time, and a periodic check catches problems early.

What happens if you skip it

  • Downtime goes unnoticed. If the site is down overnight, you may not know until a customer tells you.
  • Forms break silently. An enquiry form that stops delivering costs you leads without any visible error.
  • Security holes open. Software that is never updated becomes easier to attack.
  • Information goes stale. Old prices, discontinued products or wrong phone numbers damage trust.
  • Certificates and domains expire. An expired security certificate shows visitors a warning page.

None of these is dramatic on its own. Together they are the difference between a website that works for you and one that quietly lets you down.

What a good care plan includes

Look for specifics, not slogans. A useful plan should say:

  • How often uptime is checked and what happens when the site is down.
  • Who applies security updates and how often.
  • How content changes are handled: how many, how quickly, by whom.
  • Whether there is a regular review of how the site looks, loads and appears on Google.
  • What it costs, and how to cancel.

Support in the first months

Many builders include a support period after launch. At Phaseline, 6 months of support is included with each service. Whatever developer you choose, ask what is covered, for how long and what happens afterwards.

Can you do it yourself?

If your site has an admin panel, your team can handle content changes. Security updates, uptime monitoring and performance checks are harder to do reliably without tools and expertise. Many owners handle content themselves and ask a professional to handle the rest.

A simple checklist you can use today

  • Visit your own site on a phone and a computer. Does everything load?
  • Submit your own contact form. Does the message arrive?
  • Check your domain and certificate renewal dates.
  • Read each page for outdated facts.
  • Test your WhatsApp button.

Doing this monthly catches most problems early.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need maintenance if my website is simple?

Simple sites still need uptime checks, working forms and current content. They may need less, but not nothing.

What is an AMC for a website?

AMC means annual maintenance contract. It is an agreement for regular upkeep over a year. The details vary, so read what is covered.

How do I know if my website is down?

Use a monitoring service, or a care plan that includes it. Do not rely on customers to tell you.
