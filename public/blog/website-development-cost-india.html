How much does a business website cost in India? What actually sets the price

What drives the cost of a business website in India, why quotes differ so much, and how to compare them fairly before you hire anyone.

"How much does a website cost?" is the most common question we hear, and the honest answer is that it depends on what the website has to do. This guide explains what actually sets the price, why two quotes for "a website" can differ enormously, and how to compare them fairly. We do not publish a price list, and you will not find invented price ranges here, because any number given without knowing your requirements would be a guess.

Why quotes differ so much

Two quotes can both say "business website" and describe completely different work. The difference usually sits in a few places.

1. Template or built around your business. A template website starts from a ready-made design and swaps in your logo and text. A custom website is designed around how your business sells. Templates cost less to start and cost you later when the site cannot do what you need.

2. How many pages, and what is on them. A five-page company site, a catalogue with hundreds of products and a site with a customer login are very different amounts of work.

3. Whether there is a system behind the website. A brochure website shows information. A website with an admin panel lets your team update products, track enquiries and manage records. The second takes longer and is worth more if you need it.

4. Whether it takes payments. Selling online means a store, secure payments, order records and customer accounts. That is a larger build than an enquiry website.

5. Who writes the content and prepares the images. If you supply final text and photos, the work is faster. If the agency has to produce them, that is extra work and should appear in the quote.

6. What happens after launch. Hosting, security updates, content changes and support are sometimes included and sometimes billed separately. A low first price with expensive changes later is a common trap.

What is usually inside a fair quote

When you compare quotes, check that each one tells you:

  • What you will receive: number of pages, features, admin tools.
  • Who owns the code, the design files and the accounts at the end.
  • How long it will take, and what the milestones are.
  • What is included after launch, and for how long.
  • How payment is split, and what happens if the project is cancelled.
  • What is not included, such as photography, copywriting or payments.

If a quote is one line and a number, you cannot compare it with anything.

The questions that matter more than the number

  • Will I own it? You should own the website files, design files and logins from launch. Ask in writing.
  • Can my team update it? If every small change needs a paid request, the real cost is higher than the quote.
  • Is it fast on a phone? Most visitors will open it on a mobile network. Ask how page speed is handled.
  • Who will I talk to? Direct access to the people building it saves weeks.

A word about very cheap and very expensive

Very cheap quotes often hide costs: a template with limited ability to change, hosting you cannot move, or support that ends on launch day. Very expensive quotes often price in agency overheads such as layers of account managers. Neither is automatically wrong, but both deserve questions.

How we quote

At Phaseline we do not publish prices. Every project is quoted after a short consultation, in a clear proposal. Unless the proposal says something different, payment is in three parts: 30% as an advance before work starts, 40% when half of the work is done, and the remaining 30% before launch. A website typically takes 3 to 4 weeks, and a business system 5 to 8 weeks depending on scope. Frequently asked questions

Can I get a fixed price for a website?

Yes, once the scope is clear. A proper proposal lists exactly what is included, so the price is fixed for that scope. Changes to the scope are priced separately.

Why do agencies ask so many questions before quoting?

Because the answers decide the work. How many products, whether you need an admin panel, whether you take payments and who provides content all change the price.

Is a cheaper website bad?

Not necessarily. A small, simple website can be a good fit for a small, simple need. The risk is buying a cheap template when you actually need something that fits how you sell.

What should I prepare before asking for quotes?

Write down what you sell, who buys from you, how buyers contact you today, three websites you like and three you do not, and what you want the website to do in the first year. That makes quotes far more accurate.
